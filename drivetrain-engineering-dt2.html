DT2 design notes

DT2 uses LT1’s 1,000 mm driving wheels, 1,200 mm truck wheelbase and truck pivots at global X=3,300 and 6,000 mm. It proposes hollow steel frame members, an undrilled top support plate and wheel/axle blanks. STEP parts and the viewer derive from the same CAD solids.

Fit conflict found: the LT1 main-frame beams occupy Y=770–930 mm and Z=850–1,130 mm on either side. The DT2 sideframe reaches Y=780 mm and Z=950–1,150 mm, producing a 10 mm lateral overlap at the side members and a 50 mm overlap at the spring backing plates at straight ahead. Rotation increases the required clearance. The locomotive frame/bolster interface must change before this truck can be fitted; no accepted frame height, pivot, sidebearer or fastener pattern is established.

Frame screening: a 5,000 kg truck load allocation, 2× vertical trial multiplier and an ideal 1,200 mm side-beam span produce approximately 63.8 MPa nominal bending stress. This is a preliminary member screen, not a connection, fatigue or vehicle stability analysis. The model contains about 329 kg of frame members/plates and 1,787 kg total modeled steel per truck. Wheels dominate that mass; lightweight wheel development and an updated full locomotive mass ledger are required. Motors, bearings, springs, brakes, guards and joints are excluded.

Wheelset limits: 1,000 mm wheel blanks have a 100 mm rim width, 25 mm web and 220 mm diameter hub. The 120 mm central axle and 100 mm end blanks are proposed starting geometry. Flanges/tread profile, back-to-back, rail compatibility, press fits, axle transitions/fillets, material and heat treatment are unfinished. The CAD must not be used as a wheel-machining release.

Hydraulic interface: DT2 follows LT1’s two H1B110 motor packaging proposal, one per truck at nominal 7.5:1 total reduction. This preserves the inherited 7.50 mph equal-flow calculation with 1,000 mm wheels. Flow division, low-loop pressure, branch overspeed, motor controls, shaft reactions, charge/flush/cooling and brakes remain open; two motors do not increase available diesel power. No hose or pressure-setting schedule is issued here.

Power and structural loads follow different paths

The single-motor concept remains the development lead from 3N.2. The physical axle arrangement must fit the locomotive design; this diagram description does not select a bogie count or wheelbase.

Power path

  1. Diesel flywheel → torsionally matched coupling → H1P pump.
  2. Closed-loop A/B lines → H1B hydraulic motor.
  3. Motor coupling → separately supported reduction and distributor.
  4. Movement-compatible shafts/joints → axle final drives.
  5. Axles → driven wheels → wheel/rail traction.

Support and reaction path

  1. Engine/pump cradle transfers weight and reaction into main-frame hardpoints.
  2. Motor/reducer support carries gear reactions without assuming the motor shaft can carry unsupported side loads.
  3. Axleboxes and suspension carry vehicle loads while permitting defined wheelset movement.
  4. Nonrotating axle gearcases react torque through articulated links into the selected frame.
  5. Traction and braking loads reach the main frame and drawbar through defined guides/supports.

Each support must have one defined reference: main frame, bogie frame or axle assembly. A shaft or torque link must accommodate the actual relative motion instead of accidentally locking the suspension. Dimensions will come from the common locomotive datum and the chosen mechanism.

Pull and wheel loadingInherited 300-bar, constant-efficiency proxy: 13.47 kN pull and 4,041.5 N·m combined wheel torque. It needs 9.16 tonnes on powered wheels at assumed adhesion 0.15, or 13.74 tonnes at 0.10. These are sensitivity calculations, not measured rail adhesion or guaranteed launch performance.
Engine powerKubota lists 55.4 kW at 2,600 rpm as gross intermittent output. Installed continuous power remains unresolved. Full flow and the 300-bar launch point cannot be combined at that power budget.
Shared generator demandOwner direction is to evaluate generation from the propulsion engine first. The inherited 10 kW traction-auxiliary allowance excludes coach HVAC/hotel demand. A combined shaft-power budget must precede the engine-size decision.
Appearance and real wheelsLocomotive work uses Polar Express visual proportions. Its large visible driving wheels may conflict with the inherited 600 mm traction-wheel trial. Resolve actual powered-wheel diameter, cosmetics and turning geometry together; do not silently scale the old gearing.
Independent brakesRetain air service and spring parking. Hydrostatic retardation is not the sole stop/hold system. The locomotive brake arrangement is separate from the current thirty-two coach-chamber study.
