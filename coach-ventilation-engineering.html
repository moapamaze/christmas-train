DESIGN EVALUATION · AIRFLOW AND HARDWARE UNCONFIRMED

Heat recovery: calculation basis and selection limits.

CL2 separates recovery from a change in ventilation rate, and applies the full thermal occupancy to all four coaches. It supersedes CL1 occupancy for this evaluation; earlier reports remain historical comparisons.

Equal-flow calculation

Qvent = ρ × cp × V̇ × ΔT × (1 − ε)
Qdoor = ρ × cp × 0.03 m³/s × ΔT

ρ = 1.2 kg/m³; cp = 1.006 kJ/(kg·K); 1 m³/s = 2118.880003 CFM. Only mechanical ventilation gets recovery. ε is a net sensible-load reduction assumption, not an asserted certified SRE or raw temperature effectiveness. The temperature examples use an independent ideal 60% temperature-effectiveness assumption.

Geometry is 306 × 104 in. clear floor and a 2.2 m rectangular height proxy. Regular clear glazing is 9.910 m²; inherited ADA glazing is 6.668 m². U-values remain 1.5 W/(m²·K) opaque and 2.7 glazing, with SHGC 0.5. Solar cases retain 500 W/m² on glazing and a 20 K roof sol-air allowance. These are inherited modeling assumptions, not demonstrated shell ratings or weather design data.

All coaches use 37 thermal people at 75 W sensible and 45 W latent each. A fixed 420 W cabin-equipment heat allowance is retained in paired cases; reconcile actual fan and charger heat paths before selection. The 15% cooling allowance is applied after summing loads; winter has none. Outside humidity-ratio difference is zero in this comparison, and no latent recovery credit is claimed. Passenger latent load remains in cooling.

The airflow is still a design decision

ASHRAE’s 2023 Mass Transit chapter describes historical outside-air rates of 5–12 CFM per passenger and identifies trip length, turnover, door openings and outside contaminants as important. That range does not establish a minimum for this ride. Confirm the applicable jurisdiction/ride design criteria and operating duty, then calculate and measure delivered airflow with installed filters and ducts.

300 CFM is 8.1 CFM/person at the adopted heat allowance; 400 is 10.8 and 588 is 15.9. A 300-class unit cannot satisfy a later 400–588 CFM requirement by changing controls. CO₂ control is a boost/occupancy aid, not a substitute for the accepted minimum or exhaust-contaminant protection.

Read the recovery ratings at their test flow

ATMO300E-EC May 2026 data: heating sensible recovery is 77% at 64 CFM, 67% at 178 and 60% at 248, tested at 32°F. Cooling total recovery is 58% at 178 CFM and 95°F; it is not sensible effectiveness. No 300 CFM seasonal recovery is established here. Net airflow falls to 275 CFM at 0.6 in. w.g. The sheet requests 22 in. core-removal clearance. At ≤14°F, defrost stops supply and runs exhaust high; verify acceptable winter ventilation. Listed 6.4 A is not interchangeable with 235 W rated input.

RenewAire HE07IN has multiple motor configurations: the family’s 694 CFM maximum does not belong to every configuration. Obtain the selected unit’s fan watts, core performance, leakage and pressure losses at actual duty. Electric preheat is optional and is not included in this project ledger. Lifebreath’s brochure lists a 92% figure for 350DCS without an associated test airflow in the summary; this evaluation deliberately does not apply it at 350 CFM.

Electrical savings are operating estimates

At 300 CFM and 60% recovery, 95°F-to-75°F ventilation recovers 1.139 kW of sensible heat per coach before the 15% sizing allowance. At an assumed cooling COP of 2.5, that is about 1.82 kW less heat-pump electricity across four coaches, before any difference in fan power. At 30°F-to-68°F and heating COP 2, the corresponding reduction is 4.33 kW. These COPs are sensitivities, not selected-machine ratings or measured fuel savings.

For illustration, charging the full 0.94 kW rated ERV fan input against those benefits and crediting no plain-fan power leaves about 0.88 kW net at 95°F and 3.39 kW at 30°F, assuming the modeled recovery is achieved. An equal-flow comparison should instead subtract the actual fan-power difference. Around mild outdoor temperatures, recovery benefit declines and fan energy can dominate.

Keep the 35 kW installed continuous generator target as an unresolved procurement requirement. Do not subtract thermal recovery from connected electrical maxima. Resolve heat-pump sensible/total capacity at 75°F cabin, low-temperature heating, maximum input and start/load steps; add actual ventilator input, controls and any frost heater once. The mechanically driven brake compressor remains outside the coach electrical load, with its shaft demand still requiring selection.

Close these items before ordering

  1. Accepted ventilation duty: passenger count, ride time, boarding cycle, minimum flow and an occupied air-quality commissioning plan.
  2. Humidity balance and window surface temperatures: choose HRV versus ERV, controls, bypass and frost method.
  3. Manufacturer selection at required flow and installed static pressure, including dirty-filter allowance, fan input, recovery and air cross-leakage.
  4. Coach installation: mobile approval, restraints, service removal, condensate, weather/dust protection, intake/exhaust separation and diesel-plume checks in all operating modes.
  5. Final thermal and electrical schedule: envelope, sun, outdoor humidity, warm-up, boarding recovery and generator derating.
