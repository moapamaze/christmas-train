How the seat folds

  1. The 24-inch pan frame rotates upward around two ¾-inch hinge pins. Both end positions have metal stops.
  2. The deployed bearing lands carry downward seating load. A separate ⅝-inch positive locking pin at each side secures either end position.
  3. The fixed posts and steel floor bypass remain in the six-inch support strip. Load transfers into the underframe seat rails, around the floor panel.
  4. Proposed conversion is an empty, stationary-coach operation: one crew member controls the pan while another releases and verifies both pins. Move to the stop, fully engage both pins, fit the secondary retainers and check the locked pan before boarding.

No spring assist or automatic return is included. Final handholds, guarded crew access, secondary pin retention and the rear hinge shroud need completion. Mid-travel views demonstrate nominal geometry; they are not an operating instruction for an approved seat.

Layout and boarding

The first clear bay is X66–126 and the second X132–192 inches, both at Y−52 to −16. Support strips occupy X60–66 and X126–132. The route stays at Y−12 to +24. Companion cushions move to X86–106 and X152–172; three rear rows start at X194, X226 and X258. All dimensions share the coach datum and the current floor Z32.

The 42-inch side entry and 60-inch forward turning space are retained as reservations. The maneuvering study checks a specific 30 × 48-inch wheelchair proxy with forward/reverse motion and rear-axle turns while the other bay is occupied. Actual devices, casters, door hardware, securement and an occupied mock-up still need checking. See the path evidence and limits.
