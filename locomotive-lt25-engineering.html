Complete-train brake-failure requirements

Proposed functional specification for the system designer: one locomotive plus four loaded coaches. This is not a completed circuit or an operating procedure. Hydrostatic safety-braking credit remains zero. The current 0.5 / 0.7 m/s² service/emergency demands and 5 kNm locomotive-axle target remain study inputs.

The engineering lead must reconcile valves, reservoirs, springs, pressure thresholds, train-line continuity, controls, parking and recovery as one system. These functions cannot be inferred merely from selecting spring-brake chambers. Danfoss’s hydrostatic application manual requires independent stopping and holding capability.

Bumper decision: keep selection open

The current +25 mm contact / +30 mm steel-stop geometry leaves 5 mm working compression. No received curve qualifies a standard part for that window. ACE TA37-16 and Miner GBA-4 remain comparison candidates. Changing the engagement point or mount pocket may be necessary; neither change is released here.

ACE separates dynamic behavior above 0.5 m/s from low-speed behavior requiring a static curve. The relevant speed is local vertical motion at the bumper. Requested data includes loading and unloading curves, tolerance bands, temperature, held load, creep, stabilized cycles, lateral expansion and mounting. Selection also requires local effective mass, impact speed, load sharing and cycle rate from suspension dynamics.

The 4 kN/mm law and its 50 J loading work over 5 mm are placeholders. They are neither a measured demand nor demonstrated dissipation. Compression-bumper data does not qualify rebound-catch impact.
