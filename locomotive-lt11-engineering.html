Northern-inspired proportions: engineering consequences

This is a new proportion and running-gear concept, not a completed locomotive design. The current reference supersedes the LT10 exterior direction. DT5 and LT10 mechanical files remain intact as retained development work; their fit/performance results do not validate this new arrangement.

Drivetrain redesign required

The existing outboard DT5 motor/carrier installation would remain visible across the new driver spokes. LT11 does not conceal it by deleting necessary hardware and claiming completion. The three amber inboard volumes are reserved spaces only: no motor, reduction unit, attachment or torque path has been designed inside them. The drivetrain owner must develop a packaging solution compatible with the wheel appearance and suspension.

At equal torque per driving axle, total ideal wheel force is proportional to powered-axle count divided by wheel diameter. Changing from4×1000mm to3×1450mm gives (3/1450)/(4/1000)=0.517 of the previous force. To preserve that force requires1.933 times as much torque per driving axle. This is a ratio, not a motor selection or permission to increase pressure. Adhesion depends on the weight carried by powered wheels; pilot and trailing trucks change that distribution. Loaded train demand, curves, acceleration, speed and continuous installed engine power must be recalculated before selecting components.

The brass rod shapes are static appearance envelopes. Independent hydraulic wheelsets cannot simply be tied together with these rods. A working mechanism needs compatible phasing, bearings, axle motion, lubrication, balance and guards. The choice of a mechanically coupled or another coordinated drive arrangement remains open.

Curves and suspension

A3100mm three-driver group needs a new steering/lateral-motion design. For context only, a straight3.1m chord differs from circular arcs by about60.2mm at20m radius,40.1mm at30m, and30.0mm at40m: s=R−sqrt(R²−(L/2)²). These values are geometric sagittas, not required flange clearance or an approved minimum curve radius. Actual wheel/rail profiles, gauge clearances, axle yaw/lateral movement, rod joints, truck steering and route geometry must be resolved together.

The LT4 centre bearings and LT10 two-truck motion checks do not transfer to this architecture. Leading/trailing-truck pivots, spring equalization, bump/rebound stops, restraint and body supports are unresolved. Locomotive brakes remain with the brake owner; no coach brake geometry has been transplanted.

Engine, cooling and service access

The engine and pump stay inside the locomotive, with one shared cooling fan. Retained equipment shapes fit above the nominal wheel envelopes in the limited check performed, but the lowered installation has no verified support joints, duct design or continuous motion proof. Existing air-divider floors and panel hardware cannot be reused at new elevations without review. Engine lifting, filter/tool clearances, coupling withdrawal, exhaust/aftertreatment, thermal inlet/outlet behavior and fan removal must be rechecked with configured OEM data.

The cab floor lowers300mm with the saved seat/console geometry. Its centre roof clearance is about2003mm; side headroom, standing positions, reach, sightlines, doors and steps need an operator mock-up. The illustrated300mm-rise entry is still a placeholder. The tender structure and coach interfaces are preserved, not redesigned here.

Verification and next work

All195 nominal solids are valid; the model contains14 wheels including6 drivers and one shared fan. A limited nominal wheel/equipment intersection check found no overlap. This does not establish whole-assembly collision freedom, strength, stability, thermal performance, service access or passenger safety.

  1. Reconcile the4-6-4 appearance, powered-wheel load distribution, actual curve radius and propulsion architecture with the drivetrain owner.
  2. Select wheelsets and rail-compatible profiles; obtain real motor/reduction/coupling and brake envelopes.
  3. Develop the new inside frame, leading/trailing supports and equalized suspension using confirmed loads.
  4. Redetail equipment supports, service panels, ducts and operator station around the reviewed frame height.
