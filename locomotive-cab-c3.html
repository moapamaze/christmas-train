01 / POSITIVE LOCKING

Index pins hold the endpoints.

One cartridge is studied at each stringer. A moving disc has separate deployed and stowed holes; a sliding pin crosses two fixed cheeks. Separate stop blocks establish the travel endpoints. A slot exposes the pin head for direct position inspection. The inside-cab release and viewing route remain to be designed.

What the geometry establishes

Ø20 pins, Ø21 holes at a 100 mm radius, a 12 mm disc and two 8 mm cheeks. The pin withdraws 60 mm and clears the outer cheek by 16 mm. The two hole positions are 150.880917° apart.

All 32 modeled solids are valid. Ten sampled angles show no moving/fixed solid overlap within the isolated cartridges. The endpoint pin holes align in the nominal geometry.

What it does not establish

The cartridges have no completed frame carriers, hinge bearings, hub attachment, spring, guarded release linkage or drive. Full cab interference, continuous sweep, wear and tolerances are not checked by the isolated study.

The stated 150 kg static tread load produces about 13.81 kN at a 100 mm pin radius. Each pin is screened for the full torque; equal sharing and ground support are not credited. This is a demand, not a rating.

02 / CONTROLLED MOVEMENT

The manual drive has an exit-time cost.

The retained ladder alone weighs 41.44 kg in CAD. Its stowed position is only 0.87° beyond the gravity reversal, with a calculated energy barrier of 0.04 J. That position cannot be credited as a reliable lock. With the proposed moving discs and hubs, the moving mass becomes 51.71 kg; the peak gravity torque remains about 350 N·m and deployment releases about 654 J.

2 · Bidirectional no-backFormsprag FL-55 candidate at the input. External support bearings and couplings required.
3 · 24:1 gearboxPfaff S24 candidate. Hand operation only; application and accessory approval required.
4 · Proposed 2:1 stage48:1 overall. Final stage, hinge drive, bearings and guards still need engineering.

Forward motoring effort only. The manufacturer describes gearbox efficiency at or below 50%; the total-drive values above are explicit assumptions. They do not establish reverse efficiency, controlled descent, stopping distance or thermal capacity. No person is carried during ladder movement.

Candidate screening

The S24 lists 360 N·m output torque and 24:1 reduction. Direct drive would leave little nominal margin against 350 N·m, so the study adds an unapproved 2:1 stage. Its ideal input demand is about 175 N·m before losses and additional design loads.

The FL-55 lists 68 N·m at its own shaft. It is considered at the reduced input, not as a 350 N·m hinge brake. Its manual requires external bearing support; overhauling energy may call for an additional friction brake. Supplier application review is still required.

Decision before adoption

Twenty to forty seconds here covers cranking alone. It excludes leaving the seat, preparing the handle, releasing and confirming pins, and descending. Acceptable exit time and force have not been established.

Test the candidate on an instrumented rig, then a stationary occupied mock-up. If the complete exit sequence is not acceptable, change the access architecture or provide an independently unobstructed escape route. Do not infer an accepted emergency exit from this mechanism drawing.

03 / SEAT TO GROUND

Check the whole exit sequence.

This is a sequence for design review and a future supervised trial, not an operating instruction for unfinished hardware. The 700 ×1,900 mm opening and left controls remain as modeled in C2.

  1. Leave the restrained seat. Check release, foot placement and the nominal 600 mm cushion-to-floor difference. Verify that the left console, seat at either limit and clothing do not obstruct movement to the rear aisle.
  2. Reach the deployment controls from inside. Establish the crank location, retained handle and both lock indications while the ladder occupies the opening. No need to lean outside or stand on a moving tread should be introduced.
  3. Transfer control from locks to drive. The candidate must take the load before pin withdrawal. A partial release or one stuck pin must be detectable without forcing the ladder.
  4. Lower under control through the full sweep. The C2 sample reaches about 3.15 m from the train centre at 60°. Check the complete sweep, other track, people, ballast and ground slope. Complete the deployment unoccupied.
  5. Confirm deployed support and locks. Observe both pins and assess the ground feet before transferring body weight. Stop, pin and frame load paths require prior rig acceptance.
  6. Transfer hands and descend facing the ladder. Check the fixed-to-moving handhold transition, tread grip, foot clearance, head clearance and the final ground step. Measure total time and record assistance or loss of balance.

05 / SCOPE & EVIDENCE

Keep the study separate from the integrated baseline.

C2 remains the current integrated model. Its camera coverage, wiring, dimensioned exits, revised air routes and 504 LT32 load cases are retained. C3 adds 73.69 kg of represented lock steel across both sides and 18.02 kg of candidate gearbox/no-back device mass. Those are gross study amounts; omitted carriers, drive, guards and releases still add mass, and replaced C2 parts still need subtraction. No C3 train mass, CG or updated load-case approval is claimed.
