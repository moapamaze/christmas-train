CL2 · HEAT RECOVERY · 25 OCTOBER 2026

DESIGN EVALUATION · AIRFLOW AND HARDWARE UNCONFIRMED

Keep fresh air. Recover useful heat.

Controlled, balanced ventilation with heat recovery is worth developing for these enclosed coaches. Its strongest benefit is lower Christmas heating demand and warmer incoming air. Summer savings are useful, but do not yet justify reducing the HVAC count or the 35 kW generator target.

Heat-only HRV or moisture-recovering ERV?

An HRV transfers heat; an ERV transfers heat and moisture. For crowded Christmas trips, an HRV may better release passenger moisture and reduce window fogging. An ERV can reduce incoming moisture during humid weather, but also retains moisture when the cabin is wetter than outdoors. That tradeoff needs a coach moisture balance and humidity controls; dry desert weather alone does not settle it. NREL explanation ↗

Illustratively, a core with 60% temperature effectiveness would temper 95°F air to about 83°F with a 75°F cabin, or 30°F air to about 53°F with a 68°F cabin. These are ideal temperature examples before fan and duct effects, not product ratings.

What changes for the generator?

The old electrical ledger already contains 250 W per coach for fresh-air fans. Replacing that allowance with four 235 W rated ERVs changes the auxiliary subtotal from 4.288 to 4.228 kW; adding another 0.94 kW would count ventilation twice. Maximum input, starting VA and any frost heaters remain unconfirmed.

Keep the 35 kW target provisional. Recovery should lower operating demand, especially in winter. Final generator sizing still follows the exact heat-pump inputs, ventilator maximum input, all auxiliaries, simultaneous load steps and installed derating. The eight mini-splits remain a candidate arrangement, not a final selection.
