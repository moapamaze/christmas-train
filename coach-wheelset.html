How the wheels attach to the axles

Both wheels are pressed tightly onto one solid, rotating axle. The interference fit holds each wheel and transfers torque. The bearing locknut clamps the bearing inner ring against the axle shoulder.

The proposed retainer is an SKF KM18 locknut with an MB18 tab washer. The washer keys into the axle; one outer tab bends into a nut slot to prevent loosening. There is no castle nut in this proposal.

Build and assembly plan

  1. Release the wheelset specification with a wheelset shop. Agree rail/wheel profile, back-to-back, materials, loads, measured seat/bore tolerances and acceptable press records before machining.
  2. Machine and inspect the axle and wheels. Include shoulders, fillets, bearing fits, thread runout, washer grooves and the axle-2 brake keyway. Final heat treatment and inspection requirements belong on the released shop drawings.
  3. Stage the closed inboard brake parts before pressing the adjacent wheel. Preserve the existing split brake hub and reducer arrangement. Check the C5.5 service sequence for rotor, bearings and seal tracks.
  4. Press both wheels onto their seats. Use the approved process and support tooling; record force versus travel, set the approved wheel back-to-back, and inspect wheelset runout. Do not use the bearing nut to draw a wheel onto its seat.
  5. Mount the journal bearings, spacer, new MB18 washer and KM18 nut. Seat the inner rings against the Ø104 shoulders. Tighten using the approved bearing procedure, then bend a washer tab into a nut slot. This is not a tapered trailer-bearing back-off adjustment.
  6. Fit the axleboxes, seals and covers. Both inner rings stay clamped. The left bearing outer ring is the floating end; the right housing locates the axle. Verify free rotation, end clearances, brake clearance and the supplier’s inspection acceptance.

The connected-coach model now uses 1330 mm axles, removing 615 mm from each obsolete extension. Wheel, bearing and inboard brake locations remain at their current datums.
