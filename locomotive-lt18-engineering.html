LT18 · SEPARATE ENGINEERING RECORD

Articulated layout and tighter-curve screening

The owner selected development of eight drivers on two articulated groups. LT18 supersedes the six-driver arrangement for current locomotive development while retaining LT17 as a historical candidate. It is a packaging and planar-kinematics study. It does not establish structural capacity, a service speed, a usable minimum track radius or a fabrication release.

Geometry and physical constraints

Gauge reference 914.4 mm; driver diameter 1,450 mm; axle centres X3300,4850,6500,8050. Powered-truck pivots X4075 and7275 remain fixed to the body in plan. Each truck has two parallel axles 1,550 mm apart. The guide frames rotate with the trucks, so wheelset yaw is no longer forced through body-fixed horns.

For curve radius R, a=775 mm and half pivot spacing b=1600 mm: the truck-centre radius is rb=√(R²−a²), body chord offset d=√(R²−a²−b²), and powered-truck yaw is ±asin(b/rb). The curve centre is (X5675,±d). These placements put all four powered axle centres on the ideal centreline circle. They do not put both contact patches of each rigid wheelset on a proven running path.

The leading/trailing truck centres are nominally X1525/9975, with 1,050/950 mm wheelbases. Each centre is found by intersecting its chord-centre circle with a 1,075 mm swing-arm circle about the body anchor at X2600/8900. Thus lateral travel is constrained by a modeled arm rather than an arbitrary translation. The vertical joint, pitch/roll freedom, spring support, retention and structural capacity remain unfinished.

The 30 m target gives ±3.058° truck yaw and approximately1.480° geometric axle angle of attack. The earlier parallel three-axle layout had1.776° outer-axle attack at50 m. At the same30 m radius it would be2.962°. This comparison describes alignment only; it is not a flange-force or derailment-margin calculation.

At30 m the ideal difference in rolling radii is approximately22.10 mm, using725×914.4/R and gauge as a contact-spacing surrogate. At50 m it is13.26 mm. Tighter curves still demand more rolling-radius difference, and actual profiles, flange clearance, conicity, creepage, lubrication, gauge restraint, speed and wheel loads must be assessed. Articulation does not remove the wheel/rail problem.

Why keep30 m as the development target

The selected cylinder-support /leading-anchor gap is26.1 mm at30 m, but only9.4 mm at25 m. The selected trailing-frame /step gap is122.5 mm at30 m and55.6 mm at25 m, in both directions. These nominal closest-surface distances explain the preference for30 m; neither is a released tolerance or dynamic clearance. Selected-gap evidence.

What was checked

23 discrete planar positions: straight, plus both directions at75,50,40,35,30,27.5,25,22.5,20,17.5 and15 m. Exact BREP intersections use a0.1 mm³ reporting threshold. Every moving part is compared with fixed parts and with parts in other moving groups. Both swing arms are included. Conservative full disks replace spoke voids for all16 wheels in an additional inter-group check. The two collision counts overlap in scope and should not be added as unique physical clashes.

New main rails and crossmembers were also checked against retained equipment, cooling, cradle, station, cab, body and roof groups: no positive intersections. Parts within one moving group, general fixed-to-fixed joints, actual running railheads, nominal bearing interfaces and transmission internals are not qualified. There is no claim of a continuous swept-volume pass or tolerance/deflection allowance.

Only nominal suspension height and one illustrative crank phase are checked. No bump/rebound, roll, pitch, wheel/rail force equilibrium, hose motion, operating brakes, rotating rods, S-curves, transitions, turnouts, locomotive-to-coach connections or whole-consist swept envelope are approved. The 25 m result is the tightest clear sample in this set, not a minimum-radius specification.

Clashes resolved and boundaries exposed

The first30 m layout hit the cab step with the trailing-truck sideframe. Moving the step envelopes200 mm outboard removed that sampled collision. Their finished boarding geometry, load path, grab rails and overall route envelope must now be designed. At22.5 m the front cylinder-support extension reaches the leading swing-arm anchor. At20 m the trailing frame also reaches the step; the15 m case adds driver/main-frame and cylinder/main-frame interference. These failures remain visible in the viewer.

Frame and load paths

The initial200 mm-deep main-rail trial was rejected as an unnecessary loss of section depth. LT18 retains the nominal304.8×152.4×9.525 mm section, shifts its centres toY±240 and raises its bottom to1150/top1454.8. The outside body height and cab floor are retained. Truck sideframes top1110 leave40 mm nominal vertical packaging space below the main rails; this is not an accepted suspension or roll allowance.

Raising and moving the rails, lengthening the frame, adding two concentrated truck-support loads and replacing the crossmembers changes bending, torsion and fatigue demands. Gross section retention does not prove frame equivalence. Required load paths are body/equipment → main rails/bolsters → yaw support → truck frame → primary springs/axleboxes → wheels. Braking, traction, coupler, curve, impact and twist cases must also be included.

The360/102 mm turntable space, Ø100 kingpin and Ø102 clearances are reservation dimensions. No bearing pressure, friction moment, fastener/retainer strength, uplift restraint, tilt capability, lubrication or fatigue life is established. Primary springs have Ø140×162 mm loaded spaces only. LT16 rates, sprung-mass assumptions, stops and rebound rods are not carried forward as accepted values.

Drive strategy and mechanical side rods

One hydraulic motor per truck would drive two axle branches through a supported jackshaft. Two independent truck drives allow different truck speeds; no shaft or coupling rod bridges the articulation. At the same total tractive effort, wheel diameter and reduction, each of two motors would need1.5 times the torque of each motor in the earlier three-motor arrangement, before changes in efficiency. Existing MS05 shapes are used only to reserve space; displacement, pressure, flow, torque, thermal duty and protection must be rematched.

The21/49-tooth (2.333:1) ratio is inherited as a packaging candidate. The two green chaincase solids represent occupied space, not manufactured sprockets or a detailed jackshaft. Chain load, shaft bending, bearings, lubrication, guard containment, adjustment and movement as each axle rises must be resolved. Tsubaki’s chain-selection procedure requires transmitted power, shaft speed/diameter, centre distance and a service factor; no chain rating is accepted here.

The bronze rods show one static crank phase. Moving decorative rods still mechanically constrain wheel phase unless deliberately decoupled. They need quartering, bearings, suspension compatibility, balance, retention and full rotational clearance. Front cylinders rotate with the front truck; the piston/crosshead/main-rod animation mechanism is not yet modeled. A rigid main rod from a body-fixed cylinder to the turning truck is not part of this proposal.

Next development work

  1. Obtain actual mass/CG and axle-load targets, chosen wheel/rail profiles and route geometry; assess30 m wheel/rail forces and the full train swept path.
  2. Design the powered-truck pivot, support and positive retention, including track twist and pitch/roll freedom.
  3. Complete one axle’s primary spring, guidance, stops and rebound locks within the rotating truck. Establish independent travel before repeating it.
  4. Detail the drive, chain compensation, brakes and flexible body-to-truck hydraulic connection; check combined motion and service access.
  5. Review the new frame, truck weldments and altered supports structurally before fabrication.
