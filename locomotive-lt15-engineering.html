LT15 · SEPARATE ENGINEERING

Guide interference removed in sampled positions; curving remains unqualified

LT15 retains the LT14 body and inboard drives. It replaces the guide/header geometry and adds12 wear pads. The revised geometry is a parallel-axle lateral-motion candidate, not a radial-steering mechanism or fabrication release.

Architecture change and load paths

All three powered wheelsets have zero yaw relative to the main frame. For the left50 m geometric trial, outer axles have +12.015 mm lateral displacement and the centre axle −12.015 mm; signs reverse for the right curve. Lead and trailing truck placements remain the earlier conditional study. The driver motor, supported quill, chaincase, axleboxes and carrier move as one group, preserving motor-to-axle alignment within this rigid model.

Front/rear wear faces transfer longitudinal load and constrain yaw. Distributed guide reactions can resist the carrier’s motor torque about the axle, but actual contact pressure, edge loading, clearance take-up and structural capacity are not solved. Lateral stops react end-of-travel loads only; they do not center the axle. No centering springs or dampers have been selected. A weight-carrying suspension needs spring seats that accommodate lateral motion without imposing uncontrolled spring shear, plus bump and rebound restraint.

Why a clear model does not approve the curve

Removing radial yaw leaves approximately1.776° outer-driver angle of attack at the50 m target. Lateral clearance does not establish wheel/rail compatibility: rail profile, flange/back-to-back dimensions, effective rolling-radius difference, axle loads, gauge restraint, centering rates, speed and braking forces are needed for a vehicle-dynamics assessment. No numerical flange-climb or wheel-unloading margin is claimed.

The A1 Steam Locomotive Trust’s P2 design study describes modelling parallel coupled wheelsets, angle of attack, lateral rail forces and wheel unloading, including changes to horn clearances. It supports the need to assess forces rather than infer an operating radius from geometry. That locomotive’s dimensions or minimum radius do not validate this design.

Assumptions: sharp-corner closed rectangular sections, same steel, continuous walls and no openings. I=(bh³−bᵢhᵢ³)/12, with axes exchanged for lateral bending; J≈4Aₘ²/Σ(s/t). These are section properties, not whole-frame stiffness, allowable loads or a strength approval. Weld seams, crossmembers, local header loads, engine/body masses, braking, buff/draft loads, torsion and fatigue need review. Do not treat the narrower rail as accepted merely because it fits.

Next design gate

Resolve suspension/centering and wheel/rail dynamics together. Then size the guide contacts, stops, headers, welds and frame for the resulting loads; confirm bearing and shaft retention with suppliers. If the target curve cannot meet force limits with parallel drivers, return to a properly articulated radial arrangement or revise the wheelbase/radius requirement.
