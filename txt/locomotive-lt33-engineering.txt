The prior C2 minimum wheel load was 2.241 kN. The new nominal-curve result is 2.127 kN, so this revision receives no wheel-loading improvement credit. Each curve run still has 322 cases above the trial adhesion or brake-torque comparisons.
At the softer curve, one high-mass, twisted-track case demanding 0.7 m/s² with all coach brakes unavailable reaches the steel backup. This is a fault-demand comparison, not an achievable or approved stop. No steel reaction was calculated in the other modeled duty categories.
Fit and travel
The bumper has 40 mm nominal rubber height above its 2 mm steel plate. Proposed compression is 15 mm, or 37.5%, within the catalog's standard 40% strain plotting range. The pedestal retains its 90 mm base and the previous 80 mm upper envelope; its new internal bore is 72 mm.
The sampled cylindrical coil-corridor screen has a minimum 2.43 mm geometric gap. This excludes manufacturing tolerances and real spring deformation. A 70 mm expanded rubber diameter is reserved as a requirement to confirm, not a manufacturer rating.
The finite tilted stop edge has a minimum signed gap of -0.478 mm in an extreme brake-loss demand case. The negative gap means edge contact precedes the reduced model's centre-plane stop and must be resolved before release. Outside the all-coach-brake-loss categories the minimum sampled edge gap is 1.52 mm. Historical rebound mapping residuals also remain.
Supplier and structural checks still needed
Confirm the exact bumper: current drawing, maximum expanded shape, allowable angled contact, loading/unloading curves and repeated-impact duty for this suspension. Local moving mass and vertical impact speed remain unestablished.
Check the pedestal and attachment: 4 mm upper rim, edge load, steel grade, tapped stud retention, base joint and fatigue. The STEP model is a review proposal.
Close the complete suspension: wheel unloading, finite stop contact and the broader brake-loss / emergency-restraint cases with the running-gear designer. Prior clearance results do not automatically transfer to these new load poses.
