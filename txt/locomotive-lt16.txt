LT16 · FRONT POWERED AXLE PROTOTYPE
Supporting the locomotive while the axle moves
The front powered axle now has a proposed weight-carrying spring arrangement, lateral centering springs, bump stops and mechanical rebound retention. The whole-locomotive model keeps the motor, reduction drive, wheels, axleboxes and frame together. This is a developed installation candidate; its purchased parts and load ratings are not finalized.
Open the whole locomotive in 3D. Choose One powered axle for the complete front assembly or Spring seats & centering for the close-up. Change axle travel and roll to see the coils compress, the saddles rock and the rubber centering blocks shear. The other two powered axles retain their earlier guides; this suspension has not been repeated there.
How the connections work
Weight: each frame header loads a vertical coil spring. Its lower seat transfers weight through a sliding pad and rocker saddle into the axlebox, bearing and axle.
Sideways movement: the rocker slides under the pad while the guided lower spring seat stays aligned with the frame. The coil is not forced sideways.
Track twist: a retained pin lets the saddle rock relative to the axlebox while keeping its sliding surface level in this kinematic model.
Centering: four bonded rubber shear springs, two at each axlebox, resist lateral displacement. They are spring candidates with a stated stiffness target, not selected supplier parts. Sliding friction means return to an exact centre cannot yet be promised.
Travel restraint: guided rods have upper rebound collars. Lower strikers contact bump rubbers before reaching steel limit sleeves. Independent hooks capture the saddle if a rubber bond fails.

The usable travel study has changed
The viewer now samples −25 / 0 / +15 mm mean axle travel with −2 / 0 / +2° roll. At the individual spring seats this produces approximately −37.0 to +26.8 mm movement. Bump rubber contact starts at +20 mm locally, the steel stop is at +30 mm, and the rebound collar is at −40 mm.
The earlier −40 / +50 mm mean-travel screen used an empty spring envelope. It does not describe the travel available once these seats, spring and stops are installed. Required track-twist travel still needs route and wheel-load validation.
Frame connection changes
The main rails recover their original 152.4 ×304.8 ×9.525 mm gross section by moving inward to Y±300 mm. Their outer faces stay at ±376.2 mm, preserving the revised wheel-clearance envelope. Crossmembers shorten to447.6 mm. The front axle gains50 mm guide plates,20 mm headers and internal rail diaphragms.
The original gross rail section properties are restored, but guide-rod openings weaken the lower walls locally. Whole-frame strength and torsion are still awaiting review; this is not a structural release.
