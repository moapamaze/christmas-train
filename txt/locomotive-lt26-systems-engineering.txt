Cooling and support decisions
Proposed flow: paired side inlet → cold compartment → hydraulic cooler → removable transition → package radiator/CAC region → existing suction fan → engine bay → aft outlets. The nominal gross projected inlet area is 0.650 m² and outlet area 0.390 m². Neither is effective free area; louver blades, screens, inlet turn, cores, shroud and exits all require restriction data. No cooling capacity or opening-area ratio is accepted.
The cold plenum has a forward partition and removable floor at Z1760. Edge seals, drains and fasteners remain provisional. The cold bulkhead is body-supported with a flexible cooler seal; duct weight must not be carried by the radiator. The transition needs an independent bracket and flexible ends. This is an engineering proposal informed by Cummins T-030, ventilation/duct guidance pp201–202 and212, not approval of the Kubota/AIE package. AIE must approve its configured fan, core stack, restriction and hot-weather performance.
The new X4650 support adds a candidate load path from the aft common skid to the frame. Its two posts meet the current frame top and a lowered crossbeam; isolator spaces remain between that beam and the existing skid rails. The pump-support carrier has no assumed OEM bolt pattern or housing clamp. The extra support must be checked for load sharing and alignment rather than simply added as a rigid extra constraint.
Danfoss H1 Basic Information, pp40–44 treats overhung moment, coupling-induced radial loads and supplemental supports. Actual pump mass/CG, vibration, coupling and AIE housing limits remain necessary. This overlay does not approve any supplier flange load.
Proposed brake-failure response
 | Condition
 | Proposed function
 | Evidence needed
 | Normal service
 | BP reduction commands local friction braking from protected reserves.
 | Empty/loaded allocation, timing, torque, heat and adhesion.
 | Emergency demand
 | Remove traction; rapidly vent BP through selected emergency hardware.
 | Vent flow versus charging flow, propagation, application time and stop distance.
 | Engine / electrical power loss
 | Traction permit drops; selected failure-action hardware commands a pneumatic stop.
 | Actual de-energized valve state, independent energy and prevention of unintended release.
 | Compressor / feed-pipe failure
 | Protect local reserves; detect loss and command a stop before reserve is inadequate.
 | FP failure is not automatically BP loss. Thresholds, leakage and usable stored-air volume are open.
 | Separation / BP rupture
 | Both portions vent BP and apply from their own reserves, then remain secured.
 | Couplings and cocks must permit the intended response. Self-sealing connections or closed cocks can defeat it; continuity testing needed.
 | One coach unavailable
 | Identify fault and inhibit departure; in motion remove traction and command remaining brakes.
 | Pressure alone does not prove wheel-end force. Remaining stop, coupler loads, adhesion and heat unqualified.
 | All coach brakes unavailable
 | Remove traction and apply available independent locomotive braking; severe fault.
 | No accepted residual stopping performance or continued-service allowance.
 | Reserve depletion / parking / reset
 | Stop before reserve is inadequate; provide independent holding and controlled recharge/reset.
 | Spring application while moving, anti-compounding, release sequencing and manual recovery remain unqualified.
Architecture references: Knorr-Bremse pneumatic control describes indirect brake-pipe/distributor control. Wabtec emergency brake valve describes rapid BP exhaust. WABCO TP-0357 supports separate spring-brake/anti-compounding functions; its road-vehicle settings are not adopted. Hydrostatic safety-braking credit remains zero per the retained Danfoss application basis.
