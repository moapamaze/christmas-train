Keep fresh air. Recover useful heat.
Controlled, balanced ventilation with heat recovery is worth developing for these enclosed coaches. Its strongest benefit is lower Christmas heating demand and warmer incoming air. Summer savings are useful, but do not yet justify reducing the HVAC count or the 35 kW generator target.

Airflow reduction is a separate decision. At 95°F in the evening, changing the old 588 CFM allowance to 300 CFM changes the modeled load from 41,456 to 34,302 BTU/h before any heat recovery. Only the further reduction to 29,831 BTU/h is the recovery benefit. The higher flow may still be needed after the ventilation requirement is established.
Winter figures are occupied steady-state heating after passenger and equipment heat. They do not size an empty coach warm-up or recovery after boarding. Doors and leakage receive no recovery credit. Humidity, actual glazing, duct losses and cabin fan heat remain to be checked.
Heat-only HRV or moisture-recovering ERV?
An HRV transfers heat; an ERV transfers heat and moisture. For crowded Christmas trips, an HRV may better release passenger moisture and reduce window fogging. An ERV can reduce incoming moisture during humid weather, but also retains moisture when the cabin is wetter than outdoors. That tradeoff needs a coach moisture balance and humidity controls; dry desert weather alone does not settle it. NREL explanation ↗
Illustratively, a core with 60% temperature effectiveness would temper 95°F air to about 83°F with a 75°F cabin, or 30°F air to about 53°F with a 68°F cabin. These are ideal temperature examples before fan and duct effects, not product ratings.

This is a layout proposal; these candidates have no confirmed exposed-side or rail/mobile approval. Check supports, vibration, water protection, condensate and service removal before choosing a location. Keep all intake locations clear of engine/generator exhaust under stationary, forward and reverse operation, and separate them from the ventilation discharge.
Use a verified occupied airflow, CO₂-based boost, humidity override, airflow-failure indication and filter monitoring. Specify a bypass/free-cooling mode where supported so recovery does not warm useful cool evening air. Occupancy controls must preserve the accepted ventilation minimum. Keep the mini-splits responsible for cabin temperature and air circulation.
What changes for the generator?
The old electrical ledger already contains 250 W per coach for fresh-air fans. Replacing that allowance with four 235 W rated ERVs changes the auxiliary subtotal from 4.288 to 4.228 kW; adding another 0.94 kW would count ventilation twice. Maximum input, starting VA and any frost heaters remain unconfirmed.
Keep the 35 kW target provisional. Recovery should lower operating demand, especially in winter. Final generator sizing still follows the exact heat-pump inputs, ventilator maximum input, all auxiliaries, simultaneous load steps and installed derating.
