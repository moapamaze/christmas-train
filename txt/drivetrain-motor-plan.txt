Correction: the flange supports the motor
The MS05 male-spline motor cannot be hung from its output shaft or coupling and held only by a torque arm. Poclain's installation manual requires chassis mounting for male-spline versions. Here that means a properly supported flange carrier, with its suitability confirmed by Poclain. A generic torque-arm example does not approve this particular motor installation.
The carrier must track the axle through suspension travel. Its support carries motor weight and dynamic loads; its restraint transfers casing reaction into the truck. The coupling carries drive torque and only its rated misalignment/load. Two possible restraint concepts remain open: a guided axlebox carrier that transmits reaction through rated guides, or a separately supported articulated carrier with a reaction link. Combining both rigid constraints can bind suspension travel. We have not selected or detailed either concept.
The next mechanical release must include the carrier supports, bearing/axlebox attachment, fasteners and fits, coupling/hub, torque path, retention, guards and suspension sweep. No bracket thickness, weld or bolt torque is inferred from the motor's hole size.

Incorporation sequence
Supplier configuration: review the exact base motor, sensor and controls; obtain configured STEP and dimension drawing, mounting approval, operating map and quote.
Freeze wheelset interfaces: select railway axle/bearings/axleboxes and finalize axle-end geometry. The existing Y840 axle end and Ø160 × 140 coupling space are reservations, not compatible purchased interfaces.
Specify the mechanical assembly: procure a rated coupling with the correct DIN 5480 motor hub and a designed axle connection; resolve carrier support, reaction path and full suspension movement together. Request torsional and fatigue ratings for reversing duty.
Integrate hydraulics: matched reversible pump, charge/protection, power limiting, four speed signals and slip management, flushing, cooler, reservoir and filters. Confirm R/L mapping separately for each motor mounting side. Size work lines and independent low-pressure case drains from the selected system and actual routing.
Return the complete assembly to frame/brake coordination: mass, CG, unsprung split, dynamic reactions, hose sweeps, guards, brake envelopes and removal clearance. Check combined truck yaw, body articulation and axle travel.
Release one prototype axle: after detailed design review, fabricate and instrument one complete motor/carrier/coupling/wheelset assembly. Validate rotation agreement, creep/start, temperature, control faults and independent brakes under a reviewed test plan. Then release the other three.
