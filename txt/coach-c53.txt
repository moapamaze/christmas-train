Inboard disc brake
One right-side brake unit: a replaceable Webb rotor inside the wheel, with an axle-following caliper carrier and a short articulated torque link to the bogie frame. The commercial brake supplies the disc-clamping mechanism; the carrier, axle joint and reaction attachment are custom development parts.
Inboard disc development — unrated; supplier-fit holdsOne right-side unit with actual custom hub, split sleeves, carrier, retained torque link and backed frame mount. Nominal custom part audit and finite-link mathematics are bounded checks, not a released brake.
Actual parts and connections
The rotor turns with the axle. Pads clamp its two flat friction faces. The caliper and its carrier are restrained from following the axle’s rotation, with braking reaction carried through the short spherical-jointed link to the frame. The carrier can clock slightly as that link follows suspension movement. The carrier follows the axle’s orientation; the caliper’s wear slide is not treated as an axle-tilt joint.

Rotor dimensional basis
Webb 55030PK-65: 325.12 mm outside diameter, 34.036 mm friction thickness, 148.59 mm total U depth and 168.91 mm pilot. The published attachment is eight M14 × 2 holes on a 195.834 mm pitch circle.
The rotor section, hat clearances, pad swept band and caliper mounting relation still require the exact coordinated vendor definition. The WABCO family drawing’s 330 mm reference does not, by itself, prove this 325.12 mm rotor pairing.
Wear adjustment and suspension tracking
Commercial pad wear adjustment remains within the brake. Two spaced carrier bearing stations align the nonrotating support with the axle, and the short torque link restrains rotation while accommodating the defined suspension motion.
The final contract and check records govern bearing retention, one locating / one floating station, link articulation, wear travel, hoses and caging access. The exact 22/24 chamber’s controlled outline and force envelope remain open.
Rotor replacement requires shop wheelset service
The selected rotor is a closed one-piece part located inboard of the wheel. Replacement requires removing the wheelset and removing / reinstalling one rail wheel in a suitably equipped shop, followed by the required fit, runout and joint inspections. An on-car rotor swap or a split friction ring is not proposed.
Routine pad service is a separate access requirement. The earlier axle-end location was rejected after a real body-girder collision at combined yaw and jounce; the current study places the disc inboard.
