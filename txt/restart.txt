Current decisions and requirements
 | Area
 | Carried forward
 | Train
 | Rail-mounted diesel locomotive with historic steam-era styling; three enclosed regular coaches and one accessible coach. Custom fabrication is the primary route. The 36-inch gauge remains the working evaluation basis.
 | Regular seating
 | Retain 32 places per regular coach: sixteen two-person benches. A change needs a demonstrated fit, comfort, circulation, load or weight reason. The current compact layout has no tables; the earlier facing-bench/table concept is historical.
 | Accessible coach
 | Working layout of 11 fixed seats and two wheelchair bays. Add fold-up or removable seats for use when those bays are unoccupied by wheelchair users. Additional seat count is not yet assigned. Preserve companion access and all occupied, stowed and maneuvering envelopes.
 | Suspension & trucks
 | Develop the entire custom two-axle sprung truck, including wheelsets, bearings, axleboxes, guides, springs, damping, stops, retention, truck frame, body pivot/center bearing, sidebearers, brakes, reaction paths and service access. Show dimensioned mounts to the truck and coach frame.
 | Brakes
 | Air service and spring-applied, air-released parking. Current prototype lead is Dexter 12¼ × 4 PQ S-cam drum family with its documented Type 20/24 combination chamber. Family matching is established in the study; final rail performance and exact custom mounting interfaces are not.
 | Heating & cooling
 | Mini-split heat pumps for occupied hot fall days and Christmas heating. Indoor head high above an end door; outdoor section on the non-boarding side. A raised end roof is allowed. Exact equipment, head count and weather design points remain open.
 | Power
 | Retain diesel propulsion. Evaluate generation from the propulsion diesel before defaulting to a second generator engine. The 120/240 V split-phase recommendation is a study architecture, not selected equipment.
 | Boarding
 | Normal boarding and unloading from the same physical vehicle side at both stations. Left/right and route topology remain unassigned. Emergency egress remains a separate requirement.
 | Route
 | Owner target is 0% longitudinal grade. North Pole location is deferred; guests will disembark there. Actual route length, survey, curve radii and accepted operating speed are unresolved. Keep unrelated horse-route/county crossing details out of this train alignment.
 | Fabrication split
 | Precision work and finish work will be subcontracted. General farm/Ralph work is to be assigned against actual shop capability and the eventual released drawings.
 | Drawing standard
 | Recognizable physical assemblies, numbered parts/BOM, dimensioned plan/elevation/sections/details, materials, connections and mounts. Use one geometry source for models, drawings and parts. The owner's reference images define presentation quality only. Show commercial brakes installed on the train, without a separate internal drum teardown.
 | Delivery
 | Use this existing Site. Keep drawings, rotatable models and concise fabrication notes separate from long calculations. Continue useful independent development without repeated next-phase prompts; notify after substantive published issues. The separate track-training site remains separate.

Details that must not slip back into the design
Hydraulic service-brake preference is superseded by air service and spring parking.
Tolomatic ME220 linkage is an earlier alternative, superseded as the lead by D1 air drums. Its holes, rotor and carrier must not be mixed into D1.
596 mm wheels, 1,165 mm wheelbase, 2-in axle and the old box-shaped axleboxes/sideframes are display values, not selected fabrication sizes. The Railteco RTKZ1 example was rejected as supplied.
The earlier I1 two-bolt seat mount, floor-contacting sole and 15.775 mm spacer do not describe the current C2 four-bolt joint.
Older 24-ft/32-ft narrow coach studies, table layouts and mass estimates are history; the current C1/C2 geometry and explicit owner decisions govern continuation.
Rooftop HVAC and its trial unit counts are historical comparisons. Mini-splits now lead development; evaluate a shared propulsion/generation engine before selecting a second diesel.
The old conclusion that all work must wait for an external engineer is superseded by the owner's preliminary-design workflow. Actual missing OEM data still constrain dependent interfaces.
One mile or 6,000 ft is an earlier planning estimate. The current track quantity record has no confirmed route length. Crossings from unrelated farm plans are not a train alignment.
Exact restart sequence
C2.1 — reduce bench weight and refine mounts. Compare a lighter braced frame with realistic foot stiffness and prying/contact behavior. Reconcile metal, hardpoints, padding, finishes and small parts in the full coach mass. Preserve 32 places unless a demonstrated reason changes the layout.
C3 — accessible convertible seats. Model both wheelchair bays in occupied, seated, stowed and intermediate positions. Develop the hinge, stops, positive latches, attachment load path, storage and access before assigning extra places.
Advance the complete sprung truck. Coordinate actual wheel/axle/bearing/axlebox and D1 brake interfaces with primary suspension, guides, torque reaction, body pivot and sidebearers. Develop supported custom dimensions and identify exact remaining component data.
Finish coach integration. Resolve special end-post receivers, body/roof/panel/door/window connections, seals and mini-split supports. Update mass, CG, braking, propulsion and HVAC together.
Resume from the current source before every change, preserve predecessor issues, and publish each coherent completed issue. A recovery page or a support calculation does not complete a physical assembly.
