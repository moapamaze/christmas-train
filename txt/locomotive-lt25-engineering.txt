Complete-train brake-failure requirements
Proposed functional specification for the system designer: one locomotive plus four loaded coaches. This is not a completed circuit or an operating procedure. Hydrostatic safety-braking credit remains zero. The current 0.5 / 0.7 m/s² service/emergency demands and 5 kNm locomotive-axle target remain study inputs.
 | Condition
 | Required design response
 | Evidence needed to close
 | Normal service / emergency
 | Distributed friction braking; define traction inhibit and empty/full-load allocation.
 | Minimum guaranteed torque, application time, heat/fade, adhesion and coupler-load validation.
 | Locomotive power or brake-supply loss
 | Independent stop and hold using defined local reserve and/or spring application; a common supply loss must not disable all coaches.
 | Selected circuit, stored-energy sizing, leakage duration, low-pressure trigger and valve failure states.
 | Breakaway / severed connection
 | Both separated portions initiate the specified emergency response and remain secured without the severed connection.
 | Continuity/breakaway logic, local reserve, response-time, stop and hold tests.
 | One coach unavailable
 | Detect and identify; inhibit departure. Demonstrate the response to a failure occurring in motion.
 | Remaining-brake allocation, delay, coupler force, local wheel adhesion, stopping distance and heat.
 | All coach brakes unavailable
 | Remove traction; indicate fault; apply available independent locomotive brakes. Severe fault, not a continued-service mode.
 | Common-cause prevention plus residual stopping capability; more brake torque cannot create wheel adhesion.
 | Local reserve depleted / excessive leakage
 | Detect before reserve becomes insufficient; inhibit departure and initiate the defined stop response while usable reserve remains.
 | Minimum pressure/energy, leak-down time, monitoring coverage and fault thresholds.
 | Parking / reset
 | Hold independently of running hydrostatics; define anti-compounding and prevent unintended release during reset.
 | Holding grade/load, spring force, release logic, isolation/recovery procedure and tests.
The engineering lead must reconcile valves, reservoirs, springs, pressure thresholds, train-line continuity, controls, parking and recovery as one system. These functions cannot be inferred merely from selecting spring-brake chambers. Danfoss’s hydrostatic application manual requires independent stopping and holding capability.
Bumper decision: keep selection open
The current +25 mm contact / +30 mm steel-stop geometry leaves 5 mm working compression. No received curve qualifies a standard part for that window. ACE TA37-16 and Miner GBA-4 remain comparison candidates. Changing the engagement point or mount pocket may be necessary; neither change is released here.
ACE separates dynamic behavior above 0.5 m/s from low-speed behavior requiring a static curve. The relevant speed is local vertical motion at the bumper. Requested data includes loading and unloading curves, tolerance bands, temperature, held load, creep, stabilized cycles, lateral expansion and mounting. Selection also requires local effective mass, impact speed, load sharing and cycle rate from suspension dynamics.
The 4 kN/mm law and its 50 J loading work over 5 mm are placeholders. They are neither a measured demand nor demonstrated dissipation. Compression-bumper data does not qualify rebound-catch impact.
