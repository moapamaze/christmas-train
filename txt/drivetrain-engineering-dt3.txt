DT3 R0 design audit · 19 October 2026
Audit result: not buildable as drawn
Confirmed interference: both axle input flanges intersect their stationary gearcase necks. The basic drive topology is plausible, but the present CAD is not a working transmission. This review records defects; it does not revise or approve the mechanical geometry.
Reviewed source 14aebaa, the actual DT3 STEP components and generator, the latest shared LT3 interface return, and manufacturer data. Earlier checks proved individual solids valid and selected pairs clear; they did not establish a working assembly. The newly checked flange/housing pair was absent from the earlier test list.

Checks that support continuing this arrangement
The24/60 central reduction and 20/60 bevel reduction give7.5:1. A pitch-contact velocity check confirms both axle outputs have the same global rotation direction with the rear case turned180° aboutZ. Six sampled positions over one spur tooth pitch show no positive tooth overlap in the approximate CAD. These checks do not establish gear contact quality, life or continuous swept clearance.
Opposing suspension travel: the two axle gearboxes have separate sliding shafts and separate torque links. One axle rising while the other falls must be accommodated independently; neither axle should drag the other through the same vertical movement. The planar sample checks each branch over its own travel extremes. It does not validate combined wheelset roll/yaw, torsional oscillation, axle guidance or full truck twist.
Direction display: the current drivetrain viewer changes the camera and component visibility; it does not animate wheel or gear rotation. The direction result above is a pitch-geometry calculation, not an observed working simulation. Opposite wheel rotation viewed from the same side would be a fault; opposing suspension movement is a different requirement.
Power and traction limits
Using the same simplified efficiencies, the300-bar point gives 13.34 kN theoretical combined pull before adhesion limits. Using the engine’s gross55.4 kW as an optimistic ceiling and reserving 10 kW auxiliaries limits full-flow pressure to about 210 bar, or full300-bar speed to about 5.24 mph. Continuous installed output and shared hotel loads reduce that ceiling. At the inherited65.1 tonne mass, level track, resistance coefficient 0.005 and 2 kN extra resistance,7.5 mph needs about 35.3 kW including those auxiliaries. Thus a lower-load cruise remains plausible; route, start, curves, grades and cooling are unverified. A1% grade alone adds 6.38 kN at that trial mass.
With the inherited10 tonne adhesive-mass probe,13.34 kN requires adhesion coefficient 0.136. At0.10 the adhesion ceiling is9.81 kN. Locked axles require matched rolling diameters, accepted wheel/rail profiles and route-curving analysis. No automotive differential is present. Danfoss documents pressure-dependent low-speed stability at 15–34 motor rpm; at 7.5:1 and 1 m wheels that corresponds to about 0.23–0.53 mph. Smooth station crawl below that range needs application verification.

Power, torque and flow
The mechanism is engine → torsional coupling → H1P045 pump → two parallel H1B110 motors → one central reducer per truck → two cardan shafts → two bevel final drives → keyed axles → wheels. The engine-to-pump/generator coupling remains a separate unresolved component; DT3 does not supply its CAD.
At the inherited 300 bar differential study point,110 cc/rev and 0.90 motor torque efficiency give 472.7 N·m per motor. With0.97 efficiency per gear stage, the central output is 1146.3 N·m. Any one branch is screened for the full truck torque, not automatically half. That gives 3335.7 N·m at one axle and approximately 13.34 kN combined pull from both trucks on1,000 mm wheels. These are simplified assumptions, not pressure settings or guaranteed performance.
The inherited equal-flow case of 55.575 L/min per motor gives about 480 rpm at the motor,64 rpm at the wheels and 7.50 mph using0.95 volumetric efficiency. Parallel branches do not guarantee equal flow. One truck can take disproportionate flow; speed sensing, traction-slip control, low-loop pressure protection and pump destroking must be defined before circuit release. No flow divider is selected.
Kubota’s55.4 kW figure is gross intermittent output. Continuous installed power and the shared generator/HVAC demand remain unresolved. Full pump flow and 300 bar cannot be assumed simultaneously from this engine budget. DT3 keeps the owner’s shared-engine generation evaluation open.
Shaft motion and ratings
Nominal375 mm face spacing minus two70 mm joint offsets gives 235 mm between joint centres. With trial relative axle motion±5 mm longitudinal,±10 mm lateral and±40 mm vertical, calculated face spacing is 370.00–383.52 mm and maximum joint angle10.16°. This is inside the proposed355–425 mm length range. It assumes parallel axes and excludes wheelset pitch/yaw, bush compliance and manufacturing tolerances.
The catalog lists5.0 kN·m functional-limit torque and 1.6 kN·m reversing-fatigue torque for the shaft family. The1.146 kN·m operating branch screen and 1.719 kN·m trial shock case require a duty/angle/life calculation; the functional limit is not a fatigue approval. Correct phasing and approximately equal joint angles are required.
Each truck mechanically locks its two axles together. Match rolling diameters and assess torsional wind-up, curve creep and unloading. The two trucks remain hydraulically parallel; no differential or cross-truck shaft is implied.
Mounts, gear forces and mass
The worst-branch axle torque gives approximately15.88 kN link force with a 210 mm lever, or23.83 kN at a1.5 trial shock multiplier. The spherical-end selection target is at least 40 kN reversible axial capacity before the detailed fatigue design. Axle bearings support the gearcase and react the other moment components; the torque rod does not carry every gearbox load by itself.
The bevel tangential force screen is 19.10 kN. A preliminary Lewis calculation with assumed form factor0.30 and cone correction gives 288.2 MPa before a shock factor. No allowable stress or contact-fatigue acceptance has been established. Do not use the illustrated tooth solids for gear manufacture.
Moving the axle/spring centres to 1,400 mm increases the inherited ideal side-beam2g screen from 63.8 MPa to74.4 MPa. Four M16 through-bolts per hanger, crush sleeves and backing plates are modeled. Hanger connections, local HSS wall stress, bolts/welds, frame torsion and fatigue remain unanalysed.
Mass return: modeled geometry plus the motor allowance totals approximately2,263.4 kg per truck,63.4 kg above LT2’s 2,200 kg trial budget. This mixes nominal steel volumes and component envelopes; it is not a complete weighed BOM. Springs, axleboxes, brakes, guards, fasteners and other details are still incomplete. Lightweight wheels/gearcases and a revised locomotive mass/CG ledger need attention.
Hydraulic installation
H01 identifies both A/B loop branches and dedicated motor/pump case returns. Motor A/B interface proposal is DN 25, ISO6162 40 MPa-series split flange with M12×1.75 threads. The selected pump port variant is not assumed identical. Upper motor L1 or L2 case drain returns directly to the reservoir, without the flushing cooler in its line. Verify drain thread, pressure and routing against the final order code.
The model’s hose ends are route corridors, not verified OEM port coordinates or bent hose assemblies. Hose size, rated working pressure, end fittings, minimum bend radius, pivot slack, clamps and pressure loss remain unselected. Charge/flush filtration, cooling and reservoir sizing need an OEM-approved circuit and thermal balance. The simplified drawing omits detailed protective valve internals and settings.
Retain independent air service and spring parking brakes. Hydrostatic retardation is not the sole stop/hold system. The coach32-chamber study does not define locomotive brakes.
