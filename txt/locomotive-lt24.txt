The retained 10 kW reserve is conservatively additional to the reference fan deduction; the actual accessory schedule must separate fan, charge pump, compressor, alternator and other losses. The remaining 0.6 kW at the 35 kW trial is not an installation margin.

Quoted 13-tooth interface: a control and coupling decision

At 1,500 rpm the same 45 kW would require 286.5 Nm, above both G4 ratings and the engine’s quoted peak torque. Constant power must therefore roll off with engine speed.

A full 45 cc pump at a 360 bar pump differential would require about 286.5 Nm before charge/accessory loads, using an assumed 0.90 mechanical efficiency. The 13-tooth arrangement cannot be accepted for an unrestricted full-stroke command. Ask AIE and Danfoss to qualify either G4 with enforced torque/displacement limits or a revised G5 coupling. A larger spline alone does not supply additional engine power.
Danfoss torque figures are catalog rated/maximum categories, not a new continuous-duty approval. Shaft, coupling, lubrication, active engagement and torsional duty must be checked as one package. Published OEM cooling data also adds 42.2 kW coolant rejection and 6.0 kW turbo-aftercooler rejection at the specified 55.4 kW / 2,600 rpm reference condition; these are separate from hydraulic heat and require a revised cooling balance.

Lower speed does not remove the force shortfall at a specified deceleration. The base all-coach-loss demand at 0.5 m/s² still requires 6.34 kNm per locomotive axle and about 0.338 local adhesion. At 0.7 m/s² it requires 8.88 kNm and about 0.563. The one-coach-loss 0.7 m/s² case also exceeds the 0.15 adhesion comparison. Larger locomotive calipers alone would not remove an adhesion limit.
The design response is independently available friction braking and local stored energy on every coach, with a verified response to broken supply/control connections, loss of power, reservoir depletion and one coach being isolated. A brake pipe is a system concept; the valve circuit, emergency application and release behavior still need selection and fault testing. Hydrostatic retardation receives no independent safety-brake credit.

The bumper needs a selected curve and usable stroke
LT23 engages the bumper at +25 mm and the steel stop at +30 mm. Its 4 kN/mm linear model would reach 20 kN and store 50 J over that 5 mm interval. Stored elastic energy is not necessarily dissipated energy, and 50 J has not been established as the actual impact requirement.

Both candidates need roughly 33 mm installation height. Keeping the +25 mm engagement may require a recessed mount or a revised pedestal/striker. Installing a taller part without revising the mounting changes when contact occurs. No stop position or mount is moved in this update.
Ineffective-bumper sensitivity
Eighteen additional cases remove bumper force before the +30 mm steel stop, using the 5 mph all-coach-loss 0.7 m/s² demand as a structural sensitivity. The steel-stop reaction now reaches 2.89 kN. Across the new cases the rebound reaction reaches 7.04 kN, slightly above LT23’s 6.93 kN.
These are equilibrium forces after contact. A rigid stop calculation cannot predict its impact peak. The retained 20 kN stop and 40.91 kN single-rod probes remain review inputs, not proven capacities. Pins, brackets, guides, welds and positive locks retain their candidate dimensions pending actual material, fatigue and dynamic loads.

Supplier questions needed to close the review
Prepared technical questions for the next supplier exchange. These are requirements to resolve, not confirmations received.
AIE / Tony — installed engine and hydraulic adapter
Please reconcile the quote’s 55.4 kW “net intermittent” description with Kubota’s 52.5 kW net intermittent / 45.6 kW net continuous table. Confirm the exact engine calibration, installed fan and continuous PTO rating at 2,600 rpm, including ambient/altitude derating. Identify which accessories the rating includes, package wet mass/CG and cooling duty.
For the proposed H1P045, confirm the SAE B / 13-tooth adapter and coupling part numbers, engagement, lubrication, rated/peak torque and torsional review. Compare the 15-tooth G5 option. The current trial is 35 kW main drive plus a separate 10 kW reserve; torque limiting must account for falling engine speed and any loads passing through the same shaft.
Danfoss / Poclain — current two-motor application
The current proposal is two 560 cc motors, one per powered truck, with 4:1 reduction and 1,450 mm drivers. At 3.5 mph it needs about 97.2 L/min total at 82.4 motor rpm. Please provide exact order codes, low-speed starting/running torque and leakage maps, case/charge requirements, continuous/intermittent pressure-speed limits and approved mounting/shaft loads. Confirm the pump control can limit both power and input torque, including charge and auxiliary load, reversal and stall conditions. Earlier correspondence describing four 376 cc direct-drive motors is superseded.
Brake system lead / ZF / Webb — rated package and fault response
Confirm exact rotor, caliper, pad, chamber and control compatibility. Required data include pressure/force versus stroke with tolerances, service/parking force and anti-compounding limits, caliper transfer ratio, friction bounds, axle torque, energy/fade limits and parking duty. Identify the system designer responsible for the locomotive and all four coaches.
Define supply loss, broken connection, one-coach isolation, depleted local reserve and power-loss responses, application delay and wheel-slide behavior. The 5 kNm locomotive axle figure is a study target. The all-coach-loss demands exceed it and cannot be accepted by assuming more adhesion.
ACE / Miner — bumper selection and test data
Provide a candidate’s loading/unloading force–deflection curves through the available 5 mm working compression, including low-speed/held-load behavior, representative impact rates, temperature, creep, repeated cycles and recoverable energy. Confirm free/solid height, lateral expansion, mounting and contact-face requirements.
Selection also needs local effective mass, vertical impact velocity, parallel load sharing and cycle rate from the suspension dynamics. Train travel speed is not bumper impact speed. Assess whether the 5 mm interval can be qualified or whether engagement and mount geometry must change. Rebound-rod impact remains a separate retention calculation.
