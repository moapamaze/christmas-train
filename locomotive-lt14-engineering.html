LT14 · ENGINEERING RECORD

50 m target: body clearance improved, guidance unresolved

This overlay uses the shared LT11 exterior, LT12 inboard drive and LT13 conditional curve placements. It preserves the earlier files and coach models. It is not a completed drive system, an approved turning radius or fabrication authorization.

What changed

  • Decorative cylinders: Ø400 × 550 at X2450, Y±1000, Z1030 mm; forward caps at X2166. The lower shells and bands now stop at Z1525, versus1425 previously.
  • Main rail proposal: fabricated rectangular boxes 304.8 high ×112.4 wide ×9.525 wall at Y±320. Inside faces remain ±263.8; outside faces move to ±376.2. Top stays1350. This is a new fabricated section, not a stock-section substitution or a validated strength correction. Existing crossmember lengths remain.
  • Motor flange bolts, gussets, removable feet, through-sleeves and fastener envelopes are added. Input and output keys are nominal candidates; real spline teeth, fits and shaft/gear axial retention are still held.
  • Hydraulic free loops now sit above the next axle’s carrier cross-tie. Earlier longer loops collided there during rebound and have been superseded.

Checks use BREP intersections, with a 0.1 mm³ reporting threshold. Discrete placements are not a continuous swept-volume or vehicle-dynamics solution. Roll is a rigid-group sensitivity, not solved spring equilibrium. New weld contacts, internal moving-to-moving interfaces, hose torsion and flexible behavior, locomotive brakes, rail profiles, tolerances, wear, flex and route transitions remain outside acceptance.

The steering and torque-reaction blocker

For the 50 m ideal centreline circle, the outer driver axles need approximately ±1.776° yaw and +12.015 mm lateral offset; the middle driver needs −12.015 mm. Leading/trailing truck centres need 115.159/107.690 mm sideways movement and −4.043/+3.928° yaw. These are geometric demands, not achieved mechanism travel.

The LT12 rectangular axleboxes cannot take those motions inside the fixed straight horn guides. Simply widening the horns would remove their intended guidance and reaction contact; adding a rigid torque arm would risk further constraint. A steering-compatible axle guidance, suspension and reaction arrangement must be designed together. No cosmetic guide deletion or unrestrained sliding carrier is represented as a solution.

Connection demands—not capacity approvals

Retained LT12 trial at300bar: 1,615.7 N·m motor torque, 3,657 N·m axle torque, 2.333:1 ratio. Nominal chain tension difference at the 106.513 mm input pitch radius is about15.17 kN before chain preload, shock and reversal. Motor-flange torque alone gives approximately1.52 kN tangential load per bolt for ideal equal sharing over eight bolts at132.5 mm radius; this omits motor weight, carrier bending, preload friction and fatigue.

Nominal key shear / bearing demands at those torques are about42.7 /139.9 MPa for the18×11×70 input key and27.2 /96.7 MPa for the32×18×70 axle key using simple equal-load models. These are not allowable stresses. Shaft notch fatigue, material, fit, hub wall, reversing duty and axial retention require supplier/engineer design.

The narrower fabricated frame loses lateral bending/torsional stiffness relative to the prior section. Complete frame, cylinder bracket, carrier weld, foot, sleeve, bolt, guide and equipment-attachment loads must be recalculated. Existing engine power, mass/adhesion, brake and thermal limits have not been cleared by this fit study.

Hydraulic route evidence

Each free loop reserves691.239 mm of centreline length. The tested shapes keep that geometric length while moving the carrier end. Minimum sampled centreline bend radius is147.758 mm, minimum straight leg52.887 mm and minimum hose surface height692.720 mm above rail. These values are layout results—not hose specifications, order lengths or a proof of zero twist. End fittings, straight length beyond ferrules, impulse pressure, fluid/temperature, bend definition and manufacturing tolerance must be checked for the chosen hose.

Frame rails carry provisional A/B distribution routes and a separate case-return route toward the tank. The actual motor ports, pump/control manifold, cross-port protection, anti-slip flow management, flush/cooling circuit and case-pressure limit are not yet integrated. Dynamic hose-to-hose clearance, full roll motion, brake clearance and abrasion guards still require verification.

Manufacturer references

Poclain MS05/MSE05 technical catalogue supplies motor-family and shaft-interface references; actual configured installation approval remains required. Parker hose installation guidance requires the chosen hose’s bend limits and connection orientation to be respected. Neither reference approves this train layout.
