The reserved generator space is 1700 × 800 × 1000 mm above an 80 mm mounting allowance. This leaves 124 mm to the inner roof, 94 mm at the side lining and 25 mm to the tank partition. These are boundary gaps, not accepted service clearances. Fit of an actual 35 kW package remains to be confirmed.

Generator selection: require 35 kW continuous electrical output at the train’s installed operating conditions, 120/240 V single phase, 60 Hz. Exact model, mounting drawing, cooling and wet weight remain open. The earlier 30 kW-class EPS generator is superseded; its size and weight are not assigned to the larger unit.

35 kW adds 5 kW of capacity. The inherited four-coach study is about 26.8 kW on a hot dry evening, 31.7 kW on a hot dry day and 39.7 kW in the hot humid case. That leaves about 8.2 kW and 3.3 kW spare in the first two cases, with a 4.7 kW shortfall in the hot-humid case, before starts and added tender loads. These are thermal estimates, not selected appliance inputs. Keep essential ventilation and controls supplied; establish HVAC staging and operating limits from the actual equipment before promising full cooling in every condition.

The larger generator’s weight is unconfirmed. The prior 1.56-tonne equipment subtotal applied to the smaller reference unit and is not a current tender weight.

What this fixes in the design

The separate generator removes the need to drive a large coach alternator from the existing traction engine. The stock Hydra-Tech skid is no longer the packaging direction. LT44’s front and rear service zones and proposed frame load path remain supporting work.
