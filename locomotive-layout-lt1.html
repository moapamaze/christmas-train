01 / Dimensioned arrangement

Design basis ↗

The four large driving axles are split between two trucks. This preserves a recognizable steam-locomotive outline while shortening each rigid wheelbase. It is a proposed 2-(4+4)-4 arrangement, with a leading axle and trailing truck. It has not passed a complete curving or suspension analysis. DT2 now develops the two-motor / 1,000 mm wheel / 7.5:1 packaging basis. Its physical trucks required the frame-height revision shown in LT2; final drive hardware remains unresolved.

The reference locomotive is longer in proportion. This 15.2 m proposal compresses it to suit a farm railway and preserves operator standing room. Exact visual proportions will be refined with the powerpack, suspension and route envelope.

02 / Inspect the arrangement

Model dimensions ↗

Loading locomotive model…

Drag to rotate · pinch or scroll to zoom. Shell lift is an inspection view, not a lifting procedure. Gold/green blocks are explicitly labeled installation reservations, not manufacturer CAD. Wheels show nominal diameter and visual spokes; final tread/flange profiles are unmodeled.

What moves on a curve

Each powered truck swivels beneath the main frame; its two axles remain parallel within that truck. Leading, trailing and tender trucks also require their own yaw and suspension movement. No rigid connecting rod spans the two powered trucks.

What is inside

A ventilated decorative boiler encloses the traction diesel, pump and cooling equipment. The latest shared-engine generation study comes first. The tender reserves a utility bay that could accommodate a separate generator only if that becomes necessary, plus a low service/fuel area. The shell carries no steam pressure. Fuel, exhaust and cooling routing remain open design tasks.

03 / Parts & build sequence

Component schedule ↗

Initial assembly schedule. Purchased items are candidates; custom parts do not yet have released cutting or machining dimensions. Precision machining and finish work follow the existing plan to subcontract those trades.

Exact prices are unquoted for this configuration. The first cost package needs matched running gear, hydraulic controls, reduction gearboxes and the hotel-power installation; an invented total would hide the unresolved design.

  1. Freeze the shared geometry and load cases. Reconcile coach weight, couplers, curve radius and tender mass; check wheel loads and support travel.
  2. Develop and review running gear and frame. Define wheel/axle material, fits, bearings, suspension, retention, weld details and inspection requirements. Buy qualified wheelsets/bearings and matched brake components where practical; fabricate frame and body from developed drawings.
  3. Bench-integrate power and brakes. Match the engine adapter, pump, motors, control fail states, cooling and air system. Verify reverse, overspeed protection and traction inhibition before vehicle installation.
  4. Fixture and assemble the chassis. Use released datums, support fixtures and weld sequence. Measure frame, truck pivots and wheel loads before body panels conceal access.
  5. Install services and removable enclosures. Route hoses and wiring through the full movement envelope; verify heat barriers, ventilation, cab escape and inspection access.
  6. Qualify unoccupied, then with controlled test loads. Agree numerical limits first. Test static loads, suspension/twist, low-speed curving, service/emergency/parking brakes, both portions of every separation, cooling, repeated duty and recovery. Record results and resolve findings before passenger release.

Planned shop equipment: level assembly bed and survey instruments, rated lifting stands, qualified welding/inspection tools, specialist axle/bearing machining and NDT, hydraulic pressure/flow/temperature logging, brake pressure/timing sensors and individual wheel-load scales. Capacities and fixtures depend on the developed assemblies.
