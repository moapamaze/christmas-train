Insulated for heat and noise

Reserve 50 mm of noncombustible mineral-wool lining in the walls, removable coal-effect cover and service doors. Protect the lining with an oil-resistant acoustic facing and perforated metal inner panels. Seal panel joints with an appropriate temperature-rated system and keep insulation secured against vibration. This is a construction approach to develop, not a selected fire-rated assembly or a promised noise reduction.

  • Keep cooling passages open: engine radiator intake, engine hot-air outlet, generator base intake/discharge, and hydraulic-cooler air supply/discharge must be sized independently. Baffled vents add resistance that must be included.
  • Separate exhaust protection: exhaust pipes, DOC and mufflers need their own shields and supplier clearances; lining thickness does not substitute for those clearances. Tailpipe termination must avoid the cab and coach intakes.
  • Keep the spill floor serviceable: use a cleanable metal spill-control surface, with no exposed absorbent lining on the floor. Provide appropriate containment and controlled servicing of leaks.
  • Retain access: removable insulated panels, vibration isolation, tank inspection and an equipment removal path. The shown 600 mm aisle is a space allowance, not proof of every service operation.

ROCKWOOL engine-room acoustic facing illustrates oil-resistant acoustic protection with perforated cladding. Its marine approval does not establish approval of this train enclosure. PowerTech's airflow arrangement must remain functional inside the additional enclosure.

Earlier packaging checks and remaining work

These LT43 results apply to the earlier equipment boxes and mass allowances. They do not validate the revised exterior, reference trucks or a future combined power unit. LT43.1 changes the visible enclosure and presentation; no new structural or operating release is claimed.

The eight main equipment core boxes do not overlap within this trial body. That check excludes installation clearances, fittings, thermal performance and full suspension movement.

The partial ledger relocates 959 kg of existing machinery, tank and fluid allowances and adds the 516 kg generator candidate: 1,475 kg (about 3,250 lb) of equipment allowances before the tender frame, running gear, new compressor, panels, insulation and connections. This is not a complete tender weight. The earlier 50.6 kg electric compressor/VFD allocation is retired separately.

Driven-wheel weight falls about 6.5% in the partial static comparison. The locomotive changes from 16.81 t to 15.80 t before new connection hardware; front-truck body support drops from about 2,563 kg to 1,712 kg. All static axle reactions remain positive in this limited screen. Adhesion on wet rail, spring preload, dynamic movement and braking are not accepted by this result. Moving propulsion to the tender is a separate architecture option if the retained-drive arrangement proves unsuitable.

The new 5.8 m train-length increment must be included in the turnaround, depot and coupling checks. No previous LT42 clearance result is claimed for the inserted tender. Next: select the cooling/tank/compressor packages, complete a wet-mass ledger, resolve the flexible hydraulic interface, and rerun the coordinated movement and braking calculations.
