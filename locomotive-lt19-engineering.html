LT19 · SEPARATE ENGINEERING RECORD

Rear support, weight and movement.

The decision is to retain X4075 /7275 powered pivots for the next study and omit the trailing-wheel assembly from the candidate. The 800 mm rearward-shift alternative remains available for comparison. Neither arrangement is released for fabrication.

2 · Support assumptions and equilibrium

Two main-body supports are assumed at X4075 and7275. The leading truck carries its own weight and half the swing-arm weight in the base case. This is a proposed boundary condition requiring a suitable leading-truck suspension; LT18 has no solved load-sharing system. A three-support structure cannot be solved from vertical statics alone without stiffness/preload information.

For body masses mᵢ at xᵢ: Rᵣ=Σmᵢ(xᵢ−4075)/3200, R𝒻=Σmᵢ−Rᵣ. These reactions are reported as equivalent kilograms; forces multiply by 9.80665. Truck self-weight moments are then distributed to each pair of axles. This is why the front two axle loads differ: the cylinder support and outriggers are forward of the truck centre.

3 · Gross frame bending

The screening model is a continuous overhanging beam on the two powered pivots, with two identical rails sharing vertical load equally. Point components act at assumed CGs; long components are represented by centroid-preserving uniform spans. Actual equipment brackets, crossmember flexibility, local load introduction and torsional sharing are absent.

STI's HSS12×6×3/8 table gives 42.7 lb/ft, Iₓ 216 in⁴ and Sₓ 35.9 in³ per rail. The model uses these catalog properties and E 200 GPa, rather than optimistic sharp-corner CAD section properties. Section availability, material grade, wall loss, welds and joints remain subject to the structural design.

σ=M/(2S), with beam curvature M/(2EI). No allowable-stress, buckling, fatigue or yield acceptance is inferred. At the base mass, current pivots produce about58.3 MPa gross vertical bending stress and16.4 mm calculated downward rear-end deflection. A2× static-load sensitivity doubles those values;2 is not a selected rail dynamic factor.

4 · Curve and movement evidence

50 exact-solid cases compare the current and shifted-pivot options.21 nominal cases cover straight and both directions at50,40,35,30,27.5,25,22.5,20,17.5 and15 m for the current positions. Five nominal cases cover the shifted option.24 additional30 m cases combine each layout/direction with prescribed+25 mm jounce,−20 mm rebound and selected±1° pitch/roll.

Current pivots: no sampled exterior inter-group intersections at 30 m, including conservative full wheel disks. The nominal 25 m sample still clears;22.5 m retains the front cylinder-support/leading-anchor clash. Removing the trailer does not remove that front restriction. The full wheel disk is an envelope check, not a wheel/rail-contact model.

Both layouts fail the pivot-interface tilt check. Four intersecting interface pairs appear in each tilted pose because a flat thrust stack does not accommodate the prescribed tilt. Reported exterior clearance excludes none of these findings: the interface count is shown separately in the viewer. Body/truck pitch and roll load paths must be redesigned.

The800 mm shift brings a rear wheel under the cab floor:30 mm nominal separation,5 mm with25 mm jounce, and interference with combined jounce/tilt. It also increases powered-truck yaw and reduces the selected front-support gap at 30 m from26.1 to5.2 mm. This option is not promoted.

These are discrete, prescribed relative poses. Wheels are not forced to remain on a 3D rail surface during tilt; there is no rail/suspension equilibrium solution. Same-truck internal interfaces, chain length/tension, operating rod phases, spring stress, bumps, hoses, brakes, wheel profiles, lateral forces, track twist, S-curves, turnouts and continuous swept motion remain unqualified.

5 · Rear coupling envelope

Fifteen local envelope poses use the existing CA1 trial800 mm pin spacing and550 mm pin height, with a proposed locomotive pivot atX11050. A120×120 bar corridor and180 mm end corridors are sampled at yaw−14/−7/0/7/14° with neutral and±6° pitch/roll combinations. No intersections with the current locomotive solids were found.

This is only a local space reservation. The rear hitch bracket, drawgear and secondary retention are not modeled. The coach, brake hoses and intervehicle sweep are not included; the yaw range is not derived from the30 m route. Combined bearing tilt and the eventual joint's range remain to be checked. Coupler force transfer and braking/pitch stability are unresolved.
