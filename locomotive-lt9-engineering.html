LT9 R0 · 19 OCTOBER 2026 · PACIFIC

One shared cooling and ventilation fan

User design basis: one fan serves engine-bay ventilation, engine radiator/charge-air cooling and hydraulic oil cooling. LT9 supersedes the two-fan LT8 cooling arrangement. Separate liquid circuits remain separate. No additional engine-bay extractor or second radiator fan is specified. This is a packaging and airflow study, not a thermal or fabrication release.

Common airflow path

Forward underbody inlet → engine and pump compartment → upstream cooler cassette → downstream cooler cassette → one puller fan → common discharge header → both side louver banks. Air dividers, a removable floor and core perimeter seals prevent an uncontrolled bypass. The former roof intake is closed by split covers, and its raised hood is removed.

Shared airflow sizing

The same air passes every stage. Select one operating point that satisfies the bay temperature limit and every core at its actual inlet temperature. Add the pressure losses of the inlet protection, bay, core A, core B, collector, header and outlets at that same flow. Do not add independent fan CFM estimates or rely on a free-air fan rating.

Each stage warms the air: T_out =T_in +Q /(ρ cp V̇). The example below uses assumed ambient45°C, density1.10kg/m³, cp1005J/(kg·K), flow4.0m³/s, and heat inputs10/35/15kW. These are sensitivity values only, not Kubota/Danfoss data, a selected design ambient or a claimed cooling capacity.

Assembly, commissioning and maintenance

  1. Obtain the configured engine/CAC and hydraulic heat data, actual core order/ratings, wet weights and allowable enclosure temperatures. Purchase matched cooler and fan assemblies with installed performance data.
  2. Release the cooling supports and supplier mounts. Trial-fit cores and the single fan; preserve cleaning and core separation access.
  3. Fit removable air dividers, floor, intake protection and core seals. Resolve support penetrations and drains; keep routine engine service points accessible.
  4. Install the common header and removable side arms on independent supports with flexible sealed joints. Verify all flow passes through the intended path when access panels are closed.
  5. Prove fan removal with actual equipment isolated and supported. The half-shell opening is required; confirm rail/skin support attachments, selected connectors, tools and handling space.
  6. Test stationary at the specified hot-weather duty with all service panels closed. Log coolant, charge-air, oil and bay temperatures; air temperatures before/after each core; fan speed/input power and system pressure. Check recirculation, screen fouling allowance and local hot spots.

Purchase the fan rotor, motor, guard and heat exchangers. Fabricate only released supports, ducts and access pieces. No fan blades, core fins or protective guard are released for fabrication by these CAD shapes. Component SKUs, prices, bolt torques, weld acceptance and operating limits remain pending supplier and engineering review.
