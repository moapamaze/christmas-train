Next work, in order

  1. Define the connections: give each line a source, destination and circuit; locate the selected equipment and its ports.
  2. Correct the known route faults: join the three front branches to their own trunks, remove unused tails, and stagger the adjacent flex connections. Shortening ferrules alone will not remove the hose overlaps.
  3. Complete the installation: add the missing pump, motor, brake and coach runs, with real fittings, clamps, protection and service access.
  4. Check the movement: rerun turning and suspension travel, including hose-to-hose and fitting clearance, bend radius and available slack.

Next milestone: one coordinated routing drawing and matching 3D assembly. Every line should terminate at a labeled component or a clearly stated system boundary. Unresolved equipment interfaces must remain visible until they are actually designed.

What changed in this review

The site now has a filtered connection view and an endpoint register. Cab C2 metadata has been corrected: the BP/FP ends at X8300 are open upstream interfaces, not connections to a modeled supply. Physical route geometry, equipment and mass are unchanged. Earlier clearance studies did not establish clearance between these truck hose leads.

This is a bounded audit of the LT33 / Cab C2 / LT32 assembly, not a complete circuit inventory or installation release. The existing spring decision remains in LT35: D5 requires too much adaptation, shorter standard alternatives are being sought, and the priced spring package is still pending.
