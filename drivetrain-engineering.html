Current mounting development: DT5 powered axle engineering. This page retains earlier audits and studies.

DT4 · Engineering reference · 19 October 2026

Buy the drive, engineer the interfaces

Development choice: four low-speed hydraulic shaft motors, one per solid axle, with direct 1:1 couplings. This replaces DT3’s custom gear train. It is the preferred architecture to detail and obtain supplier confirmation for; it is not a procurement or fabrication release.

Flow, force and engine power

At 7.5 mph, 1,000 mm wheels turn at 64.03 rpm. The 376 cc study needs 101.4 L/min total. The inherited pump produces an estimated 111.15 L/min at 2,600 rpm, so it has nominal flow headroom. At 300 bar, each motor produces about 1616 N·m, giving 12.93 kN total wheel-rim force before adhesion and locomotive resistance.

The 468 cc alternative provides more torque but exceeds the present pump’s flow at 7.5 mph. That makes 376 cc the lead study candidate, not a final supplier-approved selection.

Maximum speed and maximum pull cannot be simultaneous with the proposed engine. Using 55.4 kW gross intermittent engine output minus 10 kW auxiliaries as an optimistic ceiling gives only 5.74 mph at 300 bar, or 230 bar at 7.5 mph. Installed continuous power will be lower or otherwise require an actual engine duty rating. Pump power limiting and a vehicle speed limit are mandatory design features.

These estimates use pump total efficiency 0.855, motor volumetric efficiency 0.95 and motor torque efficiency 0.90, all assumptions. They exclude hose/valve and coupling losses. At 0.25 mph the motor turns only 2.13 rpm: supplier breakaway, leakage and smooth-control data at that speed are essential. Catalog maximum speed, power and pressure are not a guarantee at every operating point.

Old 65.1 tonne train-mass sensitivity: each 1% grade adds approximately 6.38 kN of resistance. At 10 tonnes adhesive locomotive mass and adhesion coefficient 0.10, the traction ceiling is only 9.81 kN. Neither mass nor adhesion is accepted for final selection. Reconcile the latest locomotive and coach mass ledger before stating passenger or grade capacity. Calculation inputs and results.

How it attaches to the truck

The wheelset rotates in dedicated axle bearings. The motor is carried alongside the axle by a designed axlebox / carrier assembly, and a short coupling transmits torque. Motor reaction passes through that carrier and its rated guides or torque restraint to the truck. A motor fixed to the sprung frame cannot be rigidly coupled to an independently moving axle. Roll, yaw and lateral axle motion require the actual carrier kinematics or a suitably rated flexible coupling.

The CAD places a 308 mm motor envelope from Y900 to Y1208, leaving 92 mm to the nominal body side at Y1300. That is a packaging observation only. The body plane is not a route loading gauge; truck yaw, wheelset travel, hoses, fittings, brake hardware, guards and removal access have not been swept. Staggered motor positions are provisional. The old DT3 axle end at Y840 is only an inherited reference and may need redesign for the coupling.

Hydraulics and wheel direction

Use controlled parallel branches with four speed signals and supplier-engineered anti-slip control. An unrestricted parallel connection can let a slipping axle take excessive flow. Do not simply put all motors in series or lock them to equal flow without an application calculation. The two wheels on each axle are mechanically fixed together; separate axles can differ in speed.

A/B loop connections must be mapped to the actual mounting orientation so every axle propels the locomotive in the same direction. Clockwise labels viewed from opposite motor ends are not comparable. During commissioning, verify contact-direction agreement at all wheelsets under a controlled procedure. DT3’s static viewer did not prove a motion sequence.

Specify charge supply, cross-port/low-pressure protection, overspeed and pump anti-stall response, flushing, cooling, filtration and separate case drains as a system. Preserve independent air service and spring parking brakes. No brake substitution, hose size, relief setting or port thread is released by the functional drawing.

Practical build sequence

  1. Freeze duty inputs: accepted train and adhesive mass, route grade/radius, speed and continuous engine power after auxiliaries.
  2. Request a matched four-motor hydraulic package with full order codes, OEM CAD and confirmed crawl/start duty. No supplier inquiry has been sent.
  3. Specify the complete wheelset, bearings, axlebox and coupling with a rail engineer or builder; then detail the carrier and torque restraint from actual interfaces.
  4. Complete swept clearances through suspension and truck articulation, connection fatigue, thermal balance and braking/control fail states.
  5. Build and instrument one powered axle test assembly before committing to both trucks. Confirm direction, starting torque, temperature and braking under a reviewed test plan.
