01 / What the frame looks like

Dimension record ↗

Two continuous HSS12×6×3/8 main rails sit at Y±850 mm. Four deeper crossmembers occupy the proposed running-gear support stations; eight HSS8×4×1/4 crossmembers support equipment and future body attachments. The candidate outer doublers and underside pads show their real proposed thicknesses. Pad holes remain blank until the bearing, yaw and retention interfaces are agreed.

Revision driven by DT2 interference: this frame is 500 mm higher than the initial LT1 deck basis. Main-frame top is now Z1,650 mm. The cab roof envelope is provisionally Z3,800 mm; the earlier 3,400 mm overall height does not apply to this alternative. Entry steps, enclosure proportions, vertical centre of gravity and route clearances need redesign.

The frame-side skid bolt grid is defined at X2,400 / 4,500 and Y±430 / ±570 mm. These are custom chassis hardpoints. They are not the engine manufacturer’s bolt pattern. The four engine-side pad blanks remain undrilled.

03 / Suspension and load sharing

Calculation inputs & outputs ↗

DT2’s bare modeled truck already weighs 1787.2 kg, so the earlier 1,200 kg lower-gear allowance per powered truck was impossible. This revision budgets 2,200 kg per powered truck, leaving only 412.8 kg each for motors, reducers, suspension, brakes and other omitted parts. Adequacy remains unproved.

A long frame on four body supports is statically indeterminate: the weight distribution depends on frame bending, support stiffness, equipment positions and track displacement. This study represents both main rails as one equivalent beam on four compression-only springs. It develops the chassis-side demands; it does not select the bogie springs or claim wheel-by-wheel equalization.

The 12,000 kg locomotive trial consists of 6,750 kg above the body supports and 5,250 kg of assumed running gear below them. A 50 mm trial static compression sets the combined effective support rates from target supported masses of 350 / 2,800 / 2,800 / 800 kg. These rates are deliberately tuned to the earlier adhesive-weight target and are not independent validation of it.

Leading / A / B / trailing are body-to-running-gear reactions, not individual wheel forces. Lowering a support base by 25 mm is a longitudinal sensitivity. The unavailable-support cases explore loss of body support; they do not authorize running with failed hardware. The powered-A fault case transfers substantial load to the remaining body supports; individual wheel contact remains outside the model.

The level 1g nominal bending result is 8.3 MPa per main rail. This is not a frame strength approval: crossmember bending, HSS wall failures, weld fatigue, torsion, lateral loads, draft forces and local mounting loads are outside this beam model. The 2g multiplier is a sensitivity assumption, not an adopted design standard. Approximately 50 mm total model displacement is mostly trial spring compression from its unloaded reference; it is not 50 mm of frame bending.

Main-rail self mass is distributed at 46 segment centres per rail. Other modeled parts act at their individual centres; equipment allowances act at the listed X. The frame model uses catalog design section properties; CAD takeoff uses nominal walls and corner radii. Neither includes complete couplers, brakes, guards, bolts, weld metal or finished paint. The unspecified allowance must be replaced as those assemblies develop.

No roll, diagonal track twist, wheel unloading, wheel/rail contact, suspension damping, bump/rebound stops or retention loads are calculated here. Primary and secondary spring rates in series, static ride height and travel must be reconciled with DT before springs are purchased.

Support geometry handed to drivetrain development

Top of main frame Z1,650 mm; main rail bottom Z1,345.2 mm. Powered-support pad bottoms are Z1,326.15 mm with proposed 600×600 mm footprints. Support stations remain X1,200 / 3,300 / 6,000 / 8,300 mm. The front and rear pads are 400×400×12.7 mm. These planes are packaging interfaces only: bearing height, yaw axis, roll support, traction reaction, uplift retention and maintenance separation must be designed together.

The DT2 top plate is at Z1,170 mm, leaving a nominal 156.15 mm space up to the chassis pad. That is a reservation for the support stack, not a selected bearing. With relative roll/pitch limited to ±2° and relative upward motion to 40 mm, a conservative bounding-plane calculation leaves 27.6 mm between the modeled DT2 solids and the lowest LT2 steel. Relative yaw cannot increase the radial bound used in this vertical-only check. The result excludes the missing hardware, bodywork and leading/trailing running gear; it does not establish a curve radius or suspension-travel limit. Integration calculation and assumptions ↗

Buy matched rail running gear, bearings, springs/dampers and brake hardware when a supplier can substantiate the loads and movement envelope. A machined center plate alone does not provide the required suspension, lateral guidance or fail-safe retention. The articulated wheel arrangement and steam appearance remain the LT1 direction. DT2 now uses the two-motor / 1000mm wheel / 7.5:1 packaging basis; detailed drive components and route curving remain unresolved.

04 / Engine mounting and frame connections

Mount demand calculation ↗

The removable skid has two HSS4×2×1/4 runners, 2,340 mm long, and two cross ties. Four feet connect to two main-frame crossmembers through eight candidate M16 class 8.8 bolts. Each bolt passes through a proposed Ø25.4 / Ø18 × 203.2 mm sleeve, bridging the hollow crossmember so clamp force is not applied across an unsupported void.

For an illustrative 750 kg total loaded-skid mass with its centre of gravity 600 mm above the mount plane, 2g vertical + 1g longitudinal + 0.5g lateral produces about 5.83 kN maximum compression per foot. A separate net-1g uplift comparison gives 2.00 kN per bolt; direct resultant shear is 1.03 kN per bolt with equal sharing. These are demands, not allowable capacities.

OEM engine/pump/generator mass, torque reaction, isolator stiffness and mount pattern are still required. Prying, unequal sharing, vibration and fatigue can govern this joint. Bolt length, washer stack, finish, locking, preload and tightening procedure remain open. Sleeve end bearing and retention must be verified before the connection can be released.

06 / Planned assembly and inspection

Sequence for planning after the drawings and processes are released:

  1. Close the support and powerpack interfaces. Confirm actual equipment masses/CGs, spring paths and bearing/retention geometry. Use the DT2 two-motor packaging basis and resolve its motor/reduction hardware before adding torque-reaction brackets. Confirm whether hotel power uses the propulsion diesel.
  2. Qualify stock and joints. Obtain mill certificates and actual section measurements. Complete the local-wall, weld, fatigue and draft-load calculations; assign numerical tolerances, WPS and inspection requirements.
  3. Fixture the two main rails. Establish the nose, centerline and rail-top datums on a level bed. Survey rail spacing, straightness, diagonals and support planes. Set the crossmembers and document the approved tack/weld sequence.
  4. Build the removable skid separately. Machine plate holes from common datums, fit cross ties and feet, and inspect the completed bolt grid. Develop sleeve retention and full end contact before any tightening test.
  5. Fit the supported powerpack. Use the agreed OEM adapter and isolation system. Check tool access, hose bends, exhaust heat barriers and an engineered lifting/removal path. The current upward skid-removal concept requires the decorative shell to open or lift away.
  6. Measure loads before body completion. Record each wheel load, ride height and spring travel with operating fluids and crew allowance. Conduct controlled track-twist, curving and suspension-travel tests against approved limits, then inspect joints and retain records.

Required planning tools: surveyed assembly bed, suitable saw/drill or CNC plate service, calibrated bore/position measurements, qualified welding equipment and inspection, rated handling fixtures, joint-tension equipment appropriate to the chosen fasteners, and individual wheel-load scales. This issue specifies no lift-point capacity or final acceptance tolerance.

What this issue leaves open

The next frame release needs acceptance of the raised deck and support stack, revised cab/body and stability checks, complete coupler/draft structure and support load paths, suspension travel and retention, engine OEM mounts, local-wall and fatigue calculations, weld/tolerance/fastener specifications, tender structure and dimensional clearance checks through curves. Passenger-car work and DT files were preserved. No cross-chat acceptance is implied.
