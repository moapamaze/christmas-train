ENGINEERING REPORT · LT32

Combined installation, weight and connection checks

Review candidate based on both LT31 studies and retained LT30 pivot hardware. This revision reconciles the rear connection, corrects the coupler-force height and identifies the remaining mechanical and supplier interfaces.

Fabrication remains unreleased. No confirmed Patrick, brake, bumper or absorber specifications have been incorporated. The pivot’s 3.96 mm margin remains conditional. Clear geometric paths do not establish component capacity or hose suitability.

Six mass envelopes and 168 inherited duty/track combinations are solved on straight and signed 50 m curves. Spring preloads stay at the historical baseline; braking force and torque change with mass. The new solver retains the old 800 mm default for a historical regression check, which reproduces the retained result exactly, then explicitly uses 550 mm for LT32.

These are quasi-static longitudinal and prescribed lateral-acceleration comparisons, with rigid structures and trial contact laws. They do not solve coupled locomotive/coach suspension, rail yaw reactions or vertical/lateral force components from an inclined drawbar. The separately reported three-dimensional attachment loads are not fed back into these 504 cases. Leading-truck suspension and final supplier components remain outside the solved model.

The 37.191 kN duty envelope is inherited input, not a newly established four-coach train requirement. The 200 kN case remains an unapproved comparison. Nominal average pin double-shear, ear bearing and bar net-section stresses are included in the downloadable package; they are not strength checks. Rear routing and supports

Each BP/FP circuit now has a 1050 mm horizontal turning corridor and a 1200 mm vertical corridor, each with 85 mm straight end leads. A separate supported transfer carries the change of bend plane. The yaw-only carrier has a fixed boom, spindle/arm, crossrail and a telescoping spherical-ended drawbar follower reservation. Coach terminal arms attach below the front deck to the crosshead; posts sit inboard of the stair actuator support. Outer guards and fixed-pipe clamps are represented.

The vertical span also accepts residual change in projected endpoint separation. Live yaw articulation at the body end, plane alignment at the transfer and pitch/roll relief at the coach end are required. These functions are not provided by ordinary assembly swivel nuts. Port heads, guide slot/bearings, clamp attachment, friction, pressure thrust and fatigue are still mechanical interfaces; no selected hardware or pressure-rated assembly is claimed.

Rear route checking uses line capsules against oriented coach boxes and oriented support cuboids. The initial broad boxes falsely filled space around rotated members; oriented checks resolved those cases. The first trial also exposed poor transfer-port/support placement and the coach actuator region; geometry was revised before the final check. Selected endpoint blocks intentionally enclose their hose leads and are listed exclusions. Fixed elbows, terminal internals, guide internal interference, the complete joint and secondary restraints are not fully qualified.

Seven maintenance moves retain the LT31 sequence: pump aft 650 then left 1200; cooler forward 300 then up 1600; duct up 10, forward 350, then up 1390. Cooler removal precedes duct removal. Panels, seals, connections and carrier hardware must be released as declared. Local tool space does not establish worker access, lifting, disconnected-hose handling or an approved procedure.

Pivot and supplier status

The emergency restraint is unchanged from LT30. Its 3.955 mm conditional margin still uses the archived 6.586 mm movement bound and 3.459 mm tolerance stack. The new normal model does not replace that bound or approve the restraint. The 20 mm absorber stroke is still a requirement; the catch load path and failure dynamics remain open.

No confirmed supplier ratings have been introduced. The new 5.892 kN bumper demand exceeds the earlier 5.765 kN archived demand and becomes the current normal-demand reference, pending a real force–travel curve and revised contact calculation. Brake, absorber, pump and running-gear attachments retain their supplier and engineer holds.
