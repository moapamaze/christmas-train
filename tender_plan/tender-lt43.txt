Insulated for heat and noise
Reserve 
50 mm of noncombustible mineral-wool lining
 in the walls, removable coal-effect cover and service doors. Protect the lining with an oil-resistant acoustic facing and perforated metal inner panels. Seal panel joints with an appropriate temperature-rated system and keep insulation secured against vibration. This is a construction approach to develop, not a selected fire-rated assembly or a promised noise reduction.
Keep cooling passages open:
 engine radiator intake, engine hot-air outlet, generator base intake/discharge, and hydraulic-cooler air supply/discharge must be sized independently. Baffled vents add resistance that must be included.
Separate exhaust protection:
 exhaust pipes, DOC and mufflers need their own shields and supplier clearances; lining thickness does not substitute for those clearances. Tailpipe termination must avoid the cab and coach intakes.
Keep the spill floor serviceable:
 use a cleanable metal spill-control surface, with no exposed absorbent lining on the floor. Provide appropriate containment and controlled servicing of leaks.
Retain access:
 removable insulated panels, vibration isolation, tank inspection and an equipment removal path. The shown 600 mm aisle is a space allowance, not proof of every service operation.
ROCKWOOL engine-room acoustic facing
 illustrates oil-resistant acoustic protection with perforated cladding. Its marine approval does not establish approval of this train enclosure.

These LT43 results apply to the earlier equipment boxes and mass allowances. They do not validate the revised exterior, reference trucks or a future combined power unit. LT43.1 changes the visible enclosure and presentation; no new structural or operating release is claimed.
