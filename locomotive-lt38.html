LT38 · BRAKE AIR AND COACH MOVEMENT

A defined tank package and a clearer brake decision

The revised proposal uses two standard 40 L reservoirs for each vehicle’s protected reserve, plus one wet tank on the locomotive. The BA-922 was the compressor capacity candidate; LT39 rejects its assumed direct engine drive. These choices come from a stated air-demand calculation; final brake application and supplier interfaces remain open.

The previous 24.4 L local tank is too small for this study. Four brake units per vehicle, three applications at 6 bar, and rapid pressure loss require about 66 L. Two 40 L tanks provide 80 L. This is an air-volume screen, not proof of stopping performance. The model still contains only two developed brake units per coach; the intended four-station arrangement must be completed.

Air calculation you can inspect

Study basis: four service chambers per vehicle, 0.45 L final chamber volume at the 21 mm caliper stroke envelope, 1.25 L of delivery pipe and valve volume, 6 bar service pressure, 8 bar minimum starting reserve pressure and 6.5 bar reserve floor. A 20% consumption allowance is included. Initial chamber dead-air credit is deliberately zero. The 0.45 L allowance rounds upward from an informational OEM graph; it is not a certified air-consumption value.

Three applications consume 72.36 reference litres per vehicle. An 80 L pair provides about 87.92 reference litres in the ideal rapid-discharge screen, leaving about 6.76 bar after the third application. The 60 L alternative fails this rapid-discharge case even though its slower isothermal calculation passes. Reference air is at 1 bar and the same initial temperature; rapid discharge uses γ=1.4.

Pipe-volume limit: with the other assumptions unchanged, the 80 L reserve permits at most about 1.97 L of delivery-pipe and valve volume per vehicle. The selected study allowance is 1.25 L; actual bore, all four branches and valve consumption still need to be measured. A 2 L value fails this reserve screen.

The proposed 8/9 bar charging band requires selected controls that keep service and spring-release pressure within the chamber’s 8 bar working limit. It is not a commissioned setting. A lower starting pressure or eight units per vehicle invalidates the selected reserve under these assumptions. Chamber travel of 64 mm is included only as an abnormal volume sensitivity; it is not permissible PAN17 caliper travel.

At one full service application per minute across five vehicles plus a 15 L/min leakage allowance, the calculated average is 135.6 reference L/min. That needs about 542 L/min compressor delivery to stay at 25% loaded duty. BA-921 gives about 41% duty at its quoted point; BA-922 gives about 19%. Those catalog points are at 120 psi, not the proposed 9 bar cutout. Low-speed output, purge loss, spring-release charge and brake-pipe venting still need measurement or supplier data. Startup and emergency recharge time are not yet accepted.

Coach movement review

Loading the movement record…

The added tanks and fixed air runs are checked on both regular and ADA coaches against retained truck articulation: ±7° yaw, a 2° resultant body-tilt cone, primary travel −40/+35 mm, axle roll up to 2°, and 25.4 mm wear. The separate −60 mm maintenance state is also included. These are sampled geometric cases, not a continuous sweep or a new coupled train-dynamics solution. The pipes now run below the crossmembers and around the rear bolster. The screen covers declared pipe centerlines with their outside radii and tank/strap envelopes. Detailed elbows, end fittings, clamps and attachment loads still need checking.

The hose twist issue remains open. The retained C57 review found about 21.08° change in supply-hose end clocking and 14.49° in the local hose. Its collision checks do not establish that a real hose can tolerate that movement. A supplier-qualified hose arrangement or rated rotary accommodation is needed before closing the complete movement check.

Straps, tank ports and fittings remain installation envelopes. Tank mounts, load changes, drain access and all four brake stations per coach still require integration. The old LT33 suspension calculations do not accept the added equipment automatically.
