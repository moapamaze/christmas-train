Design basis and force paths

The working geometry remains 914.4 mm gauge, four powered axles with 1450 mm drivers, 1550 mm wheelbase in each powered truck and 3200 mm between powered pivot centres. The lead truck supports its own weight in this model; its body-load sharing remains unresolved.

Roll support and stop targets

Four cartridges at Y±425. Each: 750 N/mm, 8 kN nominal preload, 160 mm installed length, 170.667 mm free length; wire 18 / mean 66 /4.808 active turns. Calculated nominal shear 334 MPa; at 16 mm compression, force 20 kN and shear 835 MPa. Positive capture is 16 mm in each direction; spring force falls to zero at 10.667 mm extension.

The roll guide is 44 OD /34 ID with a 30 mm top-lip bore. Its 80 mm base flange bolts from below with 4×M8 on PCD64. A28 mm plunger stem steps to M24 for the removable keeper washer, narrow castle nut and split pin. This permits assembly through the guide’s open bottom before the cartridge is bolted to the foot. The 80 mm rocking shoe has a 5 mm sliding-liner reservation against the body land. A5 mm lower capture cap with four M6 fasteners retains the 40 mm ball through a 32 mm opening when the shoe is unloaded.

At 8 kN, average shoe pressure is 1.59 MPa. With a sliding coefficient of 0.05 /0.10 /0.20, the two shoes contribute approximately 340 /680 /1360 N·m of yaw resistance per truck at nominal preload. Liner material, attachment, contamination and breakaway friction require testing.

Primary bumper target: begin at 20 mm compression, provide a 0→20 kN curve over 5 mm, then a steel stop at 25 mm. A linear 4,000 N/mm bumper would absorb 50 J in that stroke. This is a procurement and test target; actual bump-stop material, impact velocity, repeated-event heating and hard-stop attachment loads remain open.

Traction connection and bearing demand

The main bearing remains the GE100ES-2RS dimensional candidate. A nominal 100 /90×70 bronze sleeve fits the inner ring and slides on a 90 mm pin. The two 20 mm stop collars leave ±10 mm axial catch clearance. Each collar is fixed to its cheek by a continuous circumferential weld; sizing, pin-bore machining after welding and catch-load capacity require structural review. The link eyes use nominal GE50ES-2RS geometry. Supplier bearing ratings are not treated as permissible locomotive loads.

At the 40 kN-per-truck structural force probe, the 50 /30 tubular link has nominal axial stress 31.8 MPa. A50 mm link pin in a 160 mm-clear clevis has point-load bending stress 146.7 MPa over 180 mm bearing-centre span; double-shear stress 10.2 MPa. At the additional 80 kN attachment probe, pin bending is 293.4 MPa and tube stress 63.7 MPa. Neither probe has a material/fatigue acceptance.

The 80 kN probe allows comparison above the 40 kN longitudinal case, including uncertain sleeve drag. At the inherited combined radial probe of 123.6 kN, assumed sleeve friction coefficients 0.05–0.20 imply axial drag of 6.2–24.7 kN. A seized sleeve removes this friction bound and can bind the redundant constraints; that failure case and catch loads require separate review.

The guide sleeve’s projected pressure is 19.6 MPa at the radial probe. Main-pin 90 mm gross bending is 66.9 MPa. Final bearing equivalent loads, life, grease access, pin finish, housing fits and abutments remain supplier tasks. The original body-pivot end webs now have aft lower reliefs (Xc +110..200, Z1150..1200) for swept pin hardware; corner radii, remaining weld group and local fatigue require review.

Wheel unloading and travel study

The seven-coordinate, small-angle model solves body heave/pitch/roll and each powered truck’s pitch/roll. Primary and roll springs act in compression only. Four prescribed wheelset rail-height pairs introduce uneven track; wheel reactions include spring moments and a simplified lateral guide-force split. The model omits frame flexibility, flange contact, suspension friction, damping and the actual brake/drive reaction system.

Forces shown after a stop is reached are an extrapolation of the response without that stop. They identify the need for a nonlinear stop/impact model. Non-converged cases have no reported minimum wheel force. Speeds 3 and 5 mph, rail heights 5–20 mm and the pulling/braking forces are analyst scenarios, not operating recommendations.

Travel is not adequate for every force budget. Multiple ±20 and ±40 kN cases exceed travel. Some 10 kN cases also reach a stop under the adverse mass budgets. Positive calculated wheel force does not demonstrate derailment safety. Establish measured weights, route twist, propulsion and braking demands before selecting the final spring/travel combination.

30 m clearance evidence

23 sampled exact-solid positions report 0 rigid,0 wheel-disk and 0 spring-envelope intersections. Both curve hands include ±1° pitch/roll at +25 and −20 mm primary heave; separate ±1° axle-roll probes use −10 mm heave. Spring occupancy is lofted between a tilted lower seat and fixed upper seat; the displayed helix is illustrative. Bumpers deform and are excluded from rigid collision checking.

Maximum main-bearing misalignment 3.217°; maximum link-bearing misalignment 4.548°. Maximum sampled pivot slide 0.455 mm. Minimum roll-capture reserve 8.58 mm. Catalog 6° bearing geometry is a dimensional reference only.

The checks cover new interfaces against retained geometry. They do not validate continuous swept motion, tolerances, full crank/rod motion, tire/rail contact or dynamics. The previous front interference at tighter radii remains unresolved;30 m is a development target.

Assembly and maintenance access

2 nominal main-pin extraction checks report 0 intersections. Unload and independently support the body and truck; remove the traction link, its truck-end pin/inner ring/spacers and the main-pin nut/washer/cotter, then withdraw the main pin 250 mm aft. The 160 mm clevis opening clears the 150 mm-wide pin head nominally. This is an access check; lifting points, tools and safe support procedures are not approved.

Structural review handoff

  1. Confirm service and exceptional load cases: traction, hydraulic and friction braking, buff/draft, lateral guidance, suspension impact, separation catch and jacking.
  2. Review the dropped traction bracket, crosshead end welds, rail-wall reinforcement and roll-support cantilevers. Include prying, local bending, weld access and fatigue.
  3. Qualify springs and bumpers against the stated force/travel/stress targets; resolve the flagged travel cases and select damping.
  4. Qualify both bearing sizes and the sliding sleeve, including friction, seizure, wear, sealing, maintenance and captured-ring failures.
  5. Complete axlebox traction/lateral guidance, drive reaction and brake attachments; then rerun measured-load wheel unloading and full curving dynamics.
