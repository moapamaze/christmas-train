Reduction packaging and force path

A 12/48-tooth, 31.75 mm-pitch study gives pitch diameters 122.7/485.5 mm. These circles and nominal tooth allowances fit inside the retained guard reservations, but no chain series, strand count, sprocket bore or supported jackshaft is selected. Small-sprocket life, shaft bearing reactions and suspension-related tension compensation require manufacturer review. Do not put chain side load directly into an unqualified motor shaft.

Proposed force path: motor flange/carrier → supported jackshaft → two reduction branches → axle/wheel → rail; axle guidance → truck → lower traction link → reinforced body crosshead. Axle guidance and foundation-brake reaction brackets still need complete design.

Braking proposal and demanded performance

Provide independently controlled friction stopping and parking functions, distributed across the locomotive and every coach, with local stored energy and a defined loss-of-supply response. A brake-pipe/distributor architecture is the study direction, not a completed valve diagram. Hydrostatic retardation receives no independent safety-brake credit. Coach C57 compatibility, force curves and port assignments remain unaccepted.

The locomotive procurement target is 5 kNm per powered axle across four axles. This is an assumed capacity for comparison, not a product rating. Normal distributed service 0.5 and emergency 0.7 m/s² are demands. The one-coach-loss cases add a 13.2 tonne coach; all-coach-loss demand cases remain structural sensitivities when the needed torque or adhesion is unavailable.
