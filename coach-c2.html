C2 benches & floor attachments

Regular coach · R0 · 19 October 2026 · preliminary physical design

32 places retained. This issue adds fabricated bench frames, seat/back plates and revised four-bolt steel floor mounts. The complete coach remains unfinished. Joint stiffness, fatigue, locking, upholstery and floor sealing require further development and later engineer review.

What connects to what

  1. The bench legs attach to the upper sole plates. Four bolts per foot pass through rigid steel spacers into the lower cap plate.
  2. The cap and its two side cheeks connect to the retained seat rail. The floor opening clears this steel assembly; the sole is nominally ¼ inch above the finished floor.
  3. The seat plate attaches to four tabs. The back plate uses four through-tube sleeves. Small panel fasteners and positive locking remain to be finalized.

Foot envelopes now extend ¼ inch beyond the former 20-inch seat-space box at each end: 20½ inches overall at the floor. The 32-inch pitch leaves 11½ inches between foot envelopes. The padded seating depth proposal is 15⅞ inches and needs a full-size adult comfort trial before freezing it.

Prototype fabrication order

  1. Make one bench and one representative steel floor bay first. Verify actual tube corners, frame station and floor stack.
  2. Cut and jig the tube frame and plate blanks from the matching schedule. Trial-fit the rounded tube joints and qualify the proposed weld sequence.
  3. Weld the rail cap/cheeks before fitting floor panels. Measure distortion, then finish matched spacers and align the four-hole patterns.
  4. Dry-assemble the sole/spacer/cap stack with exact hardware. Verify socket access, thread engagement, positive locking and unloaded/loaded floor isolation.
  5. Fit pan/back fasteners, insulation between dissimilar metals, retained upholstery and a nonstructural floor closeout. Those details are still open.
  6. Complete engineer-defined prototype load, fatigue, retention, comfort and evacuation checks before repeating the sixteen-bench set.

Precision machining and finish work are planned for subcontracting. General work requires demonstrated shop capability and later released drawings.
