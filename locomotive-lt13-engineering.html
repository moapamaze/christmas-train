Calculation and geometry method

Source is the saved LT11 wheel/body/frame CAD plus LT12 drive CAD at commit e9bc6bf5b0d982d60bda11e9032acdf1edb9201c. No canonical coach, wheel or drivetrain files were altered. The 36-inch gauge, 1450 mm drivers, 650/700 mm small wheels, axle spacing, and body shapes remain provisional project geometry; this check does not authenticate them as Western Train factory dimensions.

Let a=1550 mm be half the outer-driver spacing, R the track-centre radius, and u=x−5050. Sagitta s=R−√(R²−a²). Choose the frame lateral datum halfway between the driver chord and the curve at its midpoint, so y=R−√(R²−u²)−s/2. The two outer drivers then move +s/2, the middle driver −s/2. Radial driver yaw is asin(u/R). This symmetric choice minimises the maximum centreline displacement for these three axle stations; it is not a solution of wheel/rail forces or suspension equilibrium.

Leading/trailing groups use half-wheelbases b=525/475 mm and nominal pivot X1525/8475. Their chord-midpoint radius is √(R²−b²); each group is translated to y=R−s/2−√(R²−b²−u²) and yawed by asin(u/√(R²−b²)). Both axle centres then lie on the ideal centreline circle. The necessary 108–192 mm truck swing is not provided by a fixed pivot alone. A properly engineered swing/link/steering arrangement or revised vehicle geometry is needed. Pivot-space volumes are deliberately excluded from collision solids because they are reservations, not engineered mechanisms.

All axle-following LT12 parts rotate and translate with their driving axle. Horns, headers, main frame, shell, cylinders, equipment and steps remain fixed. Suspension samples are −40, 0 and +50 mm relative to the body/frame; the drawing rail-height datum is not a simulation of wheels lifting off actual rails. Springs are excluded as unselected envelopes; rods, hoses and brakes are absent. No linkage is invented to deliver these placements.

The check contains 132 moving parts and 155 fixed parts, 306,900 broad-phase pair samples across 15 positions, followed by exact BREP intersection where bounding boxes overlap. All saved input solids are valid. Intersections above 0.1 mm³ are recorded. Wheel solids and their cosmetic tire-ring solids are reported separately, so pair counts are not counts of independent physical problems. Straight baseline clashes are retained separately from newly occurring curve pairs.

Limits: sampled positions only; no continuous sweep, independent wheel travel, roll, deflection, tolerance allowance, moving-to-moving interference, railhead/contact model, flange climb, conicity, turnout, transition/S-curve, coupling-rod dynamics or operating speed acceptance. The earlier LT12 vertical screen tested drive parts against rails/crossmembers/engine/pump only; its clean result did not cover the wheel-to-shell collisions now found.

Could rigid drivers avoid the steering mechanism?

That alternative needs its own wheel/rail study. The centreline sagitta alone is 24.03 mm at 50 m and 40.07 mm at 30 m. Sharing it symmetrically gives the ±12.02 / ±20.03 mm offsets above; it does not make them flange clearance, axle float or a required specification. With fixed parallel axles, the outer-driver angle of attack would be about 1.776° / 2.962°. Actual flange faces, back-to-back, gauge widening, railhead, conicity, suspension and any lateral axle movement must be evaluated together. Removing flanges or relying on flange force is not selected here.

For scale only, pure rolling on a radial solid wheelset needs rolling-radius difference Δr≈r·g/R. With r=725 mm and provisional contact spacing g=914.4 mm, Δr≈13.26 mm at 50 m and 22.10 mm at 30 m. Actual contact spacing and wheel profile are not yet established; a lateral tread shift cannot be calculated from this without conicity/contact geometry. Separate hydraulic motors per axle do not create independent left/right wheel rotation.
